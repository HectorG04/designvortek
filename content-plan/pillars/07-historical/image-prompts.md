# Image prompts — Historical Character Art Commissions guide

Three image prompts for the historical pillar, following `content-plan/IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent for historical: **aged-paper warm spotting / foxing around the text, like an old book**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A painted twelfth-century mailed knight in three-quarter profile, head and shoulders only, the mail coif catching warm light along the cheekbone. Half of the figure is occluded by the bold typography. Behind the knight, just visible at the edge, the corner of an open illuminated manuscript page resting on a candlelit desk.
Typography:     The single word **PERIOD** in Cinzel 700, all caps, color Ink #16110D, set tight (-0.015em), occupying roughly 42% of the canvas width. The letter "E" in PERIOD has the knight's face peeking out from behind it. Letterforms sit ON the parchment surface like printed letterpress, not floating.
Kicker line:    "GETTING THE YEAR RIGHT" in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, color Crimson #B23A2A. Sits directly above PERIOD, short and quiet.
Gold accent:    A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C horizontal line beneath the kicker, separating it from the headline word.
Background:     Parchment-warm — the cover reads as a page from an old book. Cream #FAF4E2 base with subtle Parchment #F4ECD8 tone variation. The parchment shows soft aged-paper foxing in warm sepia and faint amber-brown around the edges of the typography and across the upper-left and lower-right corners. The foxing is irregular, organic, and looks like real century-old paper damage — not a Photoshop overlay. This is the historical genre accent.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8, Ink #16110D, Gold #D4A24C, Crimson #B23A2A, Cream #FAF4E2. The mailed knight's rendering uses warm earth tones, deep browns and steel-gray with warm rim light, on the parchment plane.
Composition:    Slightly asymmetric. The headline word PERIOD sits left-of-center. The painted knight emerges from behind the letter E on the right side of the composition, his profile catching candlelight from the lower right. There is breathing room at the top of the canvas above the kicker. The corner of the illuminated manuscript page peeks into frame at the lower-left edge.
Lighting:       Single warm key light from camera-right, low angle, the kind a candle on a desk would throw. Deep shadow on the left side of the knight's face. The parchment itself is lit from above with diffuse soft light.
Mood:           A scholarly evening, oil lamp burning low, the painter has been cross-referencing an old armor catalog for the last three hours and the work is almost done.
Style:          Oil painting on parchment, visible brushwork, soft edges. Frazetta meets medieval illumination meets a Penguin Classics fantasy cover. Hand-painted texture in the mail and the skin tones.
Negative:       no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter typography, no horned helmets, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light, no dragons, no lens flares, no sparkles, no stock heroic poses, no faux-weathered scroll borders, no d20 dice, no fantasy armor with non-period rivets, no costume-drama clichés, no pure black or pure white.

### Inline mid-article image (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A painted Viking-age Norse woman, head and shoulders, three-quarter view, looking off-frame to camera-left. She wears a wool apron-dress over a linen shift, with two oval tortoise brooches at the shoulders catching the warm light. Tablet-woven trim in deep red and gold runs along the linen collar. Her hair is braided and partially covered with a simple linen cap. No helmet. No horns. The portrait is plainly period-accurate, drawn from Birka and Hedeby finds.
Typography:     None.
Background:     A deep, soft-focus interior: a longhouse at evening, the suggestion of timber and woven hangings, with one cool fill from a small window upper-right and one warm key from a hearth out of frame to camera-left.
Palette:        Earth tones — desaturated linen cream, wool blue overdye, the brass-and-copper warmth of the brooches, deep ox-blood red on the tablet weave. Skin painted over a pink underlayer so she reads as fully alive, not theatrical.
Composition:    Centered subject with breathing room around the head. Her gaze leads the eye off-frame, creating quiet narrative tension. Shoulders fill the lower third. The brooches sit on the rule-of-thirds intersections.
Lighting:       Two-source: warm key from camera-left (the hearth), cool ambient fill from camera-right (the window). Single dominant warm light, with the cool only enough to keep her skin from going monochrome.
Mood:           A woman caught mid-thought at her own table, the day's work mostly done. Period-real, not theatrical. The viewer feels they have walked into her house rather than a costume museum.
Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, painterly skin rendering. The lineage is Frazetta + Vermeer + medieval reenactor photography.
Negative:       no horned helmet, no fantasy braids, no over-rendered armor, no glow effects, no magical particles, no Hollywood Viking aesthetics, no leather-and-fur barbarian costume, no smoky AI-cliché purple lighting, no pure black, no costume-drama exaggeration.

### Inline close image (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        A detail crop. A hand resting on the open page of an armor reference catalog on a candlelit desk. Beside the hand: a small museum object card with a handwritten accession number, a magnifying loupe, and the corner of a printed photograph of a twelfth-century great helm from a museum collection. Behind it, just visible, the edge of a paintbrush balanced on a jar.
Typography:     None.
Background:     A dim painter's desk at night, warm Ink-dark tones with the candle's warm circle of light isolating the central elements. Strong negative space — the right two-thirds of the frame falls into soft shadow.
Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant, Gold #D4A24C in the candlelight on the hand and the loupe brass, Parchment #F4ECD8 on the open page, a single accent of Crimson #B23A2A on the museum card's printed banner.
Composition:    The hand and the page sit lower-left, anchoring the composition. The loupe and museum card sit just above, on the rule-of-thirds line. The right side of the frame is mostly shadow, letting the candle's warm pool of light dominate. Closing-chord composition, quiet and final.
Lighting:       Single warm candle source from frame-left, low angle, the kind of light that throws long shadows from a brush handle across a desk. Deep, settled shadow on the right.
Mood:           The research has happened. The reference has been verified. The painting begins tomorrow morning. This is the moment before the brush touches canvas.
Style:          Oil painting still life, visible brushwork, soft edges. Lineage is Northern Renaissance still life + a Penguin Classics fantasy interior.
Negative:       no skulls, no ornate scrollwork, no decorative wax seals, no fantasy props, no glowing magical elements, no over-rendered chrome, no lens flares, no AI-default candle-and-book cliché composition with floating particles, no pure black, no parchment-with-rolled-edges stock-photo look.
