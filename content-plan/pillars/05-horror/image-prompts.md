# Image prompts — Horror Character Art

Three prompts for the genre-pillar piece. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **text fraying or partially dissolved at the edges, one letter has a tendril of smoke trailing off**. Tonal register is dread, not gore. Scholarly-editorial, NOT goth, NOT grimdark.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A 1924 investigator's face in three-quarter view, half-occluded by the bold word. Late-thirties or early-forties academic — soft collar, tweed lapel, a wire-rim spectacle pushed up onto the forehead. Skin slightly too pale for the warm room they are in. Eyes downcast and slightly to the left, as if reading a document just below the frame line. Hair pinned simply. One hand visible at the lower-right corner of the canvas, fingers resting on the corner of an open book, the wrist pulled back the smallest fraction — they are about to put the page down. The left pupil is fractionally larger than the right (the wrongness detail) but only at close inspection.

Typography:     The single word **DREAD** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Ink #16110D with subtle Gold #D4A24C inner illumination as if dim candlelight is catching its edges. Word occupies roughly 40% of the canvas width, weighted upper-left. The bottom edge of each letter is slightly frayed and dissolving, as if the ink itself is sublimating. The descender of the **D** at the right of the word has a thin tendril of grey-blue smoke curling off it and drifting up out of frame — this is the genre accent, painted, not Photoshop overlay.

Kicker line:    "A PAINTER'S GUIDE TO RESTRAINT" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — a 1920s university study at night. Heavy bookshelves dissolving into deep umber shadow behind the figure. A single brass parlour lamp out-of-frame upper-left throws a small warm pool of light onto the figure's face and the book corner. The wall behind is near-black with a barely-visible vertical line of warmer ink where a tall window's curtain falls. No moon. No glow. No second light source.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (the lit cheek, the page corner, the lamp's warmest pool), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of face — dominant), Gold #D4A24C (lamp pool, typography inner light, the dust on the book's spine), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text only — used once, contained).

Composition:    Asymmetric. Word DREAD weighted upper-left, kicker centred above the word. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The investigator's face is centre-right, half behind the descender of the **D**. The book corner and hand sit lower-right. The smoke tendril rises from the upper-right of the bold word to the top edge of the canvas, drawing the eye up and out — the eye loses the figure as it follows the smoke, which is the curiosity-hook.

Lighting:       Single warm key from upper-left (the unseen brass lamp). Sharp falloff. Ink-deep cool ambient on the right side of the face. Only the left cheekbone, the bridge of the nose, the upper-left corner of the book, and the inside of one knuckle are fully lit. Everything else drops two stops within an inch.

Mood:           Late October 1924, the figure has been reading for four hours and has just understood what they are reading. The painter caught the second before they put the page down. The room got colder by half a degree.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, warm rim light, deep cool shadows. Caravaggio chiaroscuro discipline applied to a 1920s academic. Penguin Classics horror line illustration energy. The typography sits painted *into* the room — gold leaf on aged paper, ink dissolving as if the page itself is decaying — never floating as a graphic overlay.

Negative:       no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light, no glowing eyes, no blood, no tentacles in frame, no full moon, no bats, no ornate scrollwork, no goth or grimdark colour palette, no neon, no lens flares, no sparkles, no Photoshop volumetric smoke, no horror-movie poster composition, no screaming, no fangs, no Halloween iconography.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A 1924 woman investigator at a small writing desk in a hotel room, seated, three-quarter view from the left. Period-correct: shirtwaist blouse with a soft tie, long wool skirt, hair pinned up loosely, a wool cardigan over the shoulders. Mid-thirties. She is not reading the document on the desk in front of her. She has just stopped reading it. Her hands are flat on the desk on either side of the page, palms down, motionless. Her face is in three-quarter, eyes looking past the document toward the wall opposite, focused on nothing in particular. The page itself is angled away from the viewer so the contents are not legible — the painting does not show us what she has seen.

Background:     Hotel room interior at night. Floral wallpaper in deep aged-cream and faded indigo, mostly lost to shadow behind her. A brass desk lamp out-of-frame upper-right throws warm pool light on her hands, the page, and the right side of her face. The left side of her face falls into cool shadow. Behind her, the corner of an iron bedstead is just visible, dissolving into umber. No window in frame.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm lamp Gold #D4A24C across her hands and the page centre, Parchment #F4ECD8 on the lit page edge and the lit cheekbone, Indigo #2C3E5C as a single faint tertiary in the deepest wallpaper shadow (used once, sparingly). Crimson is absent — this image stays cool and quiet.

Composition:    Centre-weighted on the figure, slight downward angle as if the painter is standing just behind her left shoulder. The desk and page occupy the lower third. Her face is in the upper-middle, lit but not the focal point — the focal point is the unread page and the stillness of her hands. Breathing room on the left side of the frame, where the room dies into shadow. No typography.

Lighting:       Single warm point source from upper-right (the unseen brass lamp). Sharp falloff. Her hands and the page are the brightest elements, her right cheek a stop darker, the rest two stops darker. The painting respects the physics of a single small lamp in a large dark room.

Mood:           She has just understood the document. She is not afraid, exactly. She is recalibrating. The painter is in the room with her and she has not noticed. Restraint, not horror. The kind of horror that gets quieter as you sit with it.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Caravaggio-meets-Edward-Hopper — strong chiaroscuro, single-source warm light, deep cool shadow, period-grounded realism. Penguin Classics editorial gravitas.

Negative:       no glowing document, no green light, no obvious magical effects, no tentacles, no fog, no smoke, no second figure visible, no period-incorrect details (no zipper, no plastic, no modern font on the document), no widened eyes, no clutched-throat melodrama, no skulls, no neon, no Hollywood horror tropes, no typography.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a single hand, palm down, resting on the corner of an open book on a writing desk. The hand is a woman's, slim, ink-stained at the side of the index finger. A simple silver ring on the smallest finger. The book is leather-bound and older than the rest of the room implies — the corner is worn down to soft paper, the gilt edging has dulled to a deep oxidised brass. One detail: the hand's middle finger is a quarter-knuckle longer than it should be. The proportions of the rest of the hand are correct. Most viewers will not notice on the first pass. Some will notice on the third and feel cold without knowing why.

Background:     Negative space — the desk surface fades into warm Ink and Gold shadow. A faint suggestion of the page edge beneath the hand, but the text is illegible. Upper-right corner of the frame catches the faintest rim of brass lamplight from off-canvas.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C as the lamplight warm pool, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows under the wrist and inside the book's spine, Parchment #F4ECD8 only as the faintest edge highlight on the silver ring and the gilt page edge, Crimson #B23A2A nowhere — this image stays calm and warm.

Composition:    Hand-and-book centred slightly lower-right, strong negative space upper-left. One element dominates: the small silver ring, which catches the brightest specular and gives the eye somewhere to rest. The lengthened middle finger is fully in frame but not emphasised by composition — the painting does not point at the wrongness, it allows the viewer to find it.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-right, raking across the back of the hand and the book corner. The ring catches the brightest highlight. The leather of the binding eats the light. Shadow falls deep and cool under the wrist and into the spine. Single source. Slow falloff.

Mood:           Closing chord. The hand has just settled. The painting is over. The wrongness is in the room with you and you are not sure yet.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on the leather and the desk surface, smoother glaze rendering on the skin of the hand and the silver ring. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space and the single off-detail.

Negative:       no blood, no veins glowing, no claws, no obvious transformation, no tentacle fingers, no smoke, no glow, no typography, no second hand visible, no dramatic angle, no AI-cliché "horror hand" composition, no fingernails painted unusually, no period-incorrect ring style, no chrome, no modern materials.
