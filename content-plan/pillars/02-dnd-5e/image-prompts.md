# Image prompts — The D&D 5e Player's Guide to Commissioning Character Art

Three prompts following the Design Vortex image style guide. Cover is text-first with the D&D 5e accent (a faint d20 silhouette in negative space, behind or inside one of the letters). Inline images carry no typography.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A painted half-elf warlock peeks from behind the giant capital letter — visible from the chest up on the right side of the frame, eyes catching warm candlelight, faint pact-glow at one knuckle, expression thoughtful rather than heroic. The character is half-occluded by the typography, the way a figure stands in a doorway behind a sign.
Typography:     The single word **PORTRAIT** set in Cinzel 700, weight heavy, color Ink #16110D rendered as gilded gold-leaf inlay (Gold #D4A24C catching one edge of each letter). Letter-spacing -0.015em (tight). The word fills 40% of the canvas, positioned slightly left of center. Inside the negative space of the letter "O" sits a faint d20 silhouette in the same Ink tone, barely visible — readable as a die only on a second look.
Kicker line:    "A 5E PLAYER'S GUIDE" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, color Crimson #B23A2A. Positioned above the headline, short.
Gold accent:    60×3px solid line in Gold #D4A24C immediately below the kicker, centered to it.
Background:     Parchment-warm. A page from a thick, slightly aged manuscript, cream paper with subtle natural fiber texture, warm afternoon light raking in from the upper-left. No weathered scroll borders, no faux-parchment overlays — just a clean illuminated-manuscript page tone.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8, Ink #16110D, Gold #D4A24C, Crimson #B23A2A, with one small touch of Indigo #2C3E5C in the warlock's collar.
Composition:    Kicker top-center small, then the gold rule, then the huge **PORTRAIT** word set tight beneath, slightly left of center. The painted warlock figure emerges from behind the right edge of the "T", half-occluded so we see her shoulder, jaw, and one eye clearly. Inside the "O" the d20 silhouette sits quiet in negative space. Slight asymmetry — the eye keeps scanning between the painted figure and the die hidden in the type.
Lighting:       Single warm key light from upper-left, falling across the parchment and the right side of the warlock's face. Cool ambient fill in the deep shadows. The gold leaf on the letters catches the same light source as the figure — the typography and the painting share a sun.
Mood:           A scholarly book that has just been opened on a desk. The candle that lights the room is not in frame but is doing all the work. Editorial gravitas, not D&D kitsch.
Style:          Oil painting on parchment, visible brushwork on the character, gilded illuminated-manuscript typography, soft edges, Frazetta + Klimt + medieval illumination. The character is rendered with hand-painted texture — no 3D-render gloss, no beauty-filter skin.
Negative:       no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no Old English typography, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light, no dragons, no lens flares, no magical particles, no sparkles, no stock fantasy poses, no sword raised to the sky, no d20 rendered as a literal prop, no dice clipart, no dungeon doors, no character classes in identifying armor — keep her ambiguous enough to read as any warlock player

### Inline mid-article image (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A painted 5e party portrait fragment — three characters caught in a candlelit tavern interior, mid-conversation. Foreground: a tiefling rogue with small subtle horns, hood half-down, leaning across a wooden table. Mid: a dwarf cleric in worn plate, holy symbol catching the lamp light, looking at the rogue with patient amusement. Background, slightly out of focus: a half-elf wizard sketching something in a notebook, ink-stained fingers visible. Three characters, one room, one light source, one shared world.
Typography:     None.
Background:     Tavern interior — dark wood beams, a hearth fire just out of frame casting warm light from the right, deep shadows on the left side of the composition. Hints of leaded windows in the far background, no detail. No d20 props, no character sheets on the table — just two ceramic cups and the open notebook.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (warm tabletop highlights), Ink #16110D (deep shadows), Gold #D4A24C (firelight on metal and skin), Crimson #B23A2A (one detail only — the tiefling's belt sash), Indigo #2C3E5C (the wizard's robe in the background).
Composition:    Vertical 4:5 frame. Eye enters at the rogue's hands on the table (lower third), moves up to the cleric's face (middle), then drifts back to the wizard in soft focus (upper third). Strong diagonal of the table edge cuts the frame. Negative space behind the wizard's shoulder lets the eye rest.
Lighting:       Single warm key light from right (the unseen hearth), cool ambient blue-grey fill from left. The rogue's face is half-rim-lit, the cleric is fully lit from the right, the wizard sits in the cooler ambient with only a touch of warm on his notebook page. One sun, three faces, three different reads on the same light — the way light works in a real room.
Mood:           The middle of a long campaign session, somewhere past hour two. The party knows each other well enough not to fill silences. The painting catches them mid-thought, not mid-action.
Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, warm rim light, Frazetta + Klimt + tavern-interior Dutch-golden-age sensibility. Hand-painted textures on cloth, leather, ceramic, paper. Faces are specific and lived-in, not idealized.
Negative:       no typography, no captions, no weapon raised, no fight scene, no magical effects visible, no glowing eyes, no glowing runes on the cleric's symbol, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light, no lens flare, no sparkles, no d20 dice on the table, no character sheets, no obvious modern objects, no beauty-filter faces, no heroic stock poses

### Inline close image (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        A detail crop — one hand resting on the pommel of a longsword laid flat across a wooden table, the other hand holding a small open notebook with a one-line character pitch written in ink, half-legible. The angle is over-the-shoulder, slightly above. Gauntlet visible at the wrist, scarred knuckles, a single ring on the index finger. The notebook page shows partial cursive text, intentionally just blurred enough not to read fully — the suggestion of a written character brief, not a literal one.
Typography:     None. The cursive on the notebook page is painted as ink stroke texture, not legible type.
Background:     The wooden table fills the lower half, deep shadow above and behind, only the edge of a candle's warm halo entering from upper-right. A small pewter ink pot is just visible at the upper-left edge, mostly cropped.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (the notebook page), Ink #16110D (the deep table-edge shadow), Gold #D4A24C (warm candlelight on the steel pommel and the ring), Crimson #B23A2A (a single drop of sealing wax on the notebook page, the only saturated color in the composition).
Composition:    Square, intimate crop. The longsword cuts a strong diagonal from lower-left to upper-right. The hand on the pommel anchors the lower-right third. The notebook sits in the upper-left third, page open at a slight tilt. Negative space dominates the upper-right corner where the candle halo fades into deep ink-dark. Strong asymmetric composition — one element dominates each quadrant, with the candle glow pulling everything together.
Lighting:       Single warm key from upper-right (the candle, out of frame). Deep cool shadow on the opposite side of every surface. The gold catches on the sword pommel, the ring, the curve of the gauntlet, and the edge of the notebook page. Three small specular hits across the composition, no more.
Mood:           A closing chord. The moment just before a player sends the brief — the character is on the page, the sword is on the table, the room is quiet. The painter has already started thinking about the painting before the email lands.
Style:          Oil painting, tight detail crop, visible brushwork on the steel and the leather of the gauntlet, parchment painted with hand-laid fiber texture, candlelight rendered with soft warm halation rather than hard sparkle. Frazetta + medieval still-life sensibility. Dutch-golden-age intimacy.
Negative:       no typography overlay, no readable text in the notebook (only painted ink-stroke suggestion), no logo, no watermark, no d20, no dungeon doors, no fantasy clichés, no magical glow on the sword, no glowing runes, no AI-cliché purple-orange lighting, no lens flare, no sparkles, no second light source, no character face visible
