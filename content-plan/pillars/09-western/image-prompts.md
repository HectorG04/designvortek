# Image prompts — Western & Weird West Character Art

Three prompts for the genre-pillar piece. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **a dust-mote light shaft cutting diagonally across the cover, behind the typography**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A weathered gunslinger's face in three-quarter view, half-occluded by the bold word. Late-1870s era — wide-brimmed soft-crowned hat low over the eyes, leather cord chin-strap, a heavy moustache, sun-cracked skin, eyes catching a low warm sun. One gloved hand visible at the lower-right of the canvas, resting on the hammer of a long-barrel Colt Single Action Army in a high-rise holster. The hat brim shadow falls hard across the bridge of his nose.

Typography:     The single word **FRONTIER** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Gold #D4A24C with subtle Ink #16110D inner shadow as if cast iron type pressed into parchment. Word occupies roughly 42% of the canvas width, positioned slightly left-of-centre vertically, weighted toward the upper-middle third. The gunslinger's face peeks from behind the right side of the "N" and "T" — the typography clearly sits in front of him, with the dust shaft passing behind both.

Kicker line:    "A PAINTER'S GUIDE TO THE WEIRD WEST" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — the inside of a stable or weathered barn at the end of day. Deep umber wood-grain walls in heavy shadow. A single horizontal shaft of late-afternoon sun cuts diagonally across the frame from upper-left to lower-right, behind the typography, lighting suspended dust motes. The shaft is the genre accent — wide, warm, almost amber, with visible individual particles catching the light along its edge.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker accent line and gold leaf detail only), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of face), Gold #D4A24C (typography, dust-shaft warm core, hat-band light), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, faint blood-rust glint on the revolver's frame).

Composition:    Asymmetric. Gunslinger's face is offset to the right third, looking past the typography toward something off-frame upper-left. Bold FRONTIER word weighted left, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The dust-mote shaft passes diagonally behind everything, anchoring the composition and giving the eye a path from upper-left light source down to the gun hand in the lower-right.

Lighting:       Single warm key from upper-left (the dust shaft is the visual evidence of it), Ink-deep cool ambient fill on the right side of the face. Sharp falloff. The hat shadow does most of the work on the upper face. Edge of the dust shaft just clips the gunslinger's cheekbone — the only place his face is fully lit.

Mood:           Late summer afternoon, end of day, the barn doors are open behind him and he is standing in the only beam of light left in the building. He is not posing — he is just standing there, and the painter caught him.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, warm rim light, deep cool shadows. Frazetta-meets-Andrew Wyeth — period-grounded realism rather than pulp fantasy. Visible canvas tooth. The dust shaft rendered as painted atmosphere, not a Photoshop volumetric.

Negative:       no Stetson "Cattleman" crease (era-wrong for 1870s), no purple-orange dual rim light, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no neon, no sparkles, no glowing eyes, no horse silhouette in the background, no spinning revolver, no AI-cliché "epic western" lens flares, no flying coat or duster mid-action, no Hollywood squint, no smoke from the barrel.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A Deadlands huckster, mid-1880s, three-piece wool suit gone to seed — waistcoat unbuttoned at the bottom, shirt collar dark with sweat, frock coat draped over the back of a chair behind him. Late-thirties, beard trimmed two weeks ago and now growing back, eyes that are doing none of the work his mouth is. He is mid-shuffle on a deck of playing cards, hands lit clearly, the face in slightly softer focus than the hands. One card has fallen face-up on the table in front of him — the Queen of Spades — and it is the only saturated colour element in the composition.

Background:     Saloon interior at the end of a long night. A kerosene lantern out of frame on the upper-left throws warm pool light on his hands and the centre of the table. Beyond him, the room dies into deep shadow — only a faint suggestion of a back wall, a half-glass of whiskey, the corner of another chair.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm lantern Gold #D4A24C across hands and table centre, Crimson #B23A2A on the queen-of-spades' lips and on a thin rim of light catching the edge of the whiskey glass. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as the night-air tone in the deepest background — used once, sparingly, as a single tertiary accent.

Composition:    Center-weighted, slight downward angle as if the painter is sitting across the table from him. The fallen card sits in the lower third, drawing the eye in. His face is in the upper-centre, lit but not the focal point — the hands and the card are. Breathing room around the figure. No typography.

Lighting:       Single point source from upper-left (the unseen lantern). Sharp falloff. Hands are the brightest element in the painting, face is a stop darker, everything else two stops darker. Classic lantern-light rendering — the painting respects the physics of a small flame in a large dark room.

Mood:           He has just lost a hand he should have won. He is shuffling because he doesn't know what else to do with his hands. Something supernatural is sitting just out of frame, and he knows it.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Caravaggio-meets-frontier — strong chiaroscuro, deep shadow, single-source warm light.

Negative:       no glowing cards, no green energy, no obvious magical effects, no neon, no LED-blue, no chrome, no Hollywood squint, no spread-out fan of cards on the table, no second character visible, no typography, no lens flares.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a gloved hand resting on the grip of a long-barrel Colt Single Action Army, holstered in a Slim Jim-style holster on a worn leather gun belt. The thumb sits on the hammer, not the trigger. The glove is well-used buckskin, dust-coloured, with a small split along one knuckle. The revolver's grip is plain walnut, oiled dark by years of handling, with a single brass tack pinned into the lower curve — the kind of personalisation a working gunfighter might add. Beyond the hand and the gun, the canvas falls into dust-warm shadow.

Background:     Negative space — late-afternoon dust haze in warm Gold and Ink tones. The character is implied off-frame upper-right; you can just see the bottom of a long duster coat draped behind the belt line.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C as the dust-haze tone, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows under the holster and inside the belt loop, Parchment #F4ECD8 only as the faintest edge highlight on the revolver's frame, Crimson #B23A2A nowhere — this image deliberately stays calm.

Composition:    Hand-and-gun in the lower-right third. Strong negative space upper-left. One element dominates: the brass tack on the grip, which catches the brightest highlight in the frame and gives the eye somewhere to rest.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-right, raking across the gun and the glove. The brass tack catches the brightest specular. The leather of the holster eats the light. Shadow falls deep and cool under the gun belt.

Mood:           Closing chord. The character is just standing there, between things. The painting is over.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on the leather and the glove, smoother rendering on the brass tack and the gun's frame. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space.

Negative:       no fingers on the trigger, no spinning, no draw-mid-action, no smoke, no glow, no typography, no second hand visible, no dramatic angle, no AI-cliché "hero's hand" composition, no chrome or modern-pistol detailing — this is an 1870s revolver, not a movie prop.
