# Image prompts — character-art-commission-pricing

Three image slots per the Design Vortex IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE. Cover is text-first; inline images carry no typography. All three live in the "Cartographer's Hand" aesthetic: scholarly fantasy, illuminated manuscript, candlelit study. Oil-painting brushwork, no AI-cliché lighting, no skulls, no scrollwork.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A brushed-gold ledger lies open on a dark oak study desk, candlelight catching the gilded edge. A painter's slim hand, mid-sentence, hovers with a quill above a half-filled column of figures in iron-gall ink. The ledger's right-hand page has a wax-free, ungarnished column of neat handwritten numbers descending toward the spine. A small painter's palette with smudges of ochre, ox-blood, and bone white rests at the lower-left corner of the desk, half-occluded by the typography.
Typography:     The single word "COST" in Cinzel 700, set in deep Ink #16110D with subtle gold-leaf highlights on the inner counters of the C and the O so it reads as gilded letterpress on aged parchment. The word occupies roughly 42% of the canvas, sitting slightly off-center to the right, letter-spacing -0.015em. The "T" descends so its base touches the open ledger page, integrating the word into the scene rather than floating above it.
Kicker line:    "WHAT A COMMISSION ACTUALLY" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, in Crimson #B23A2A, placed directly above the headline word.
Gold accent:    60×3px gold line in #D4A24C sits immediately under the kicker, centered on the headline.
Background:     Parchment-warm. The desk surface is dark oak warmed by candlelight from the upper-left; the wall behind fades into deep Ink shadow. Faint warm Parchment #F4ECD8 glow surrounds the ledger, suggesting a single beeswax candle just off-frame to the left.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8, Ink #16110D, Gold #D4A24C, Crimson #B23A2A, Cream #FAF4E2 for the ledger page tone.
Composition:    Asymmetric. The ledger anchors the lower third running diagonally from lower-left to mid-right. The word "COST" sits in the upper-right two-thirds, half-occluded at its lower edge by the open ledger. The hand and quill enter from the right edge, just touching the descender of the T. Eye travels from kicker down to gold line, into the word, down into the ledger, out along the hand.
Lighting:       Single warm key from upper-left at low angle, candlelight quality, color temperature around 2400K. Deep falloff into Ink shadow on the right. Rim light catches the gold leaf on the headline and the brushed-gold edge of the ledger spine.
Mood:           A studio painter doing the bookkeeping after hours. The candle has been burning a while. The math is honest, the numbers are tidy, and the painter is still working.
Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, scholarly fantasy editorial. Frazetta + Klimt + medieval illumination. Penguin Classics fantasy line meets The Atlantic feature cover.
Negative:       no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light, no dragons, no lens flares, no sparkles, no stock fantasy poses, no d20 dice, no calculator or modern bookkeeping props, no pure white, no pure black, no neon, no cheap drop shadows on the typography.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Place after the H2 "Party portraits and the composition surcharge" or "VTT tokens vs full portraits" — whichever has more text directly above.

Subject:        A close cropped study of a painter's hand holding a small wooden palette, mid-mix. Four pools of color sit on the palette: a warm ox-blood red, a desaturated bone-and-ochre skin tone, a soft moss green, and a single bright drop of gold leaf pigment. The other hand holds a thin sable brush, tip just touching the gold pool. The hands are partially in shadow; the palette catches the light.
Typography:     None.
Background:     Ink-dark. A faint warm glow from a single candle just off-frame, low in the bottom-right of the canvas. The wall behind is a deep umber-black, painterly, with visible brush texture suggesting plaster.
Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant. Accent colors lifting off the palette: Gold #D4A24C, Crimson #B23A2A, a soft moss green for tertiary. Skin tone of the hands kept in a quiet Parchment #F4ECD8 warmth.
Composition:    Centered on the palette and brush. Hands enter from lower-left and upper-right, framing the painter's tools. Breathing room around the edges; the palette is the only fully lit element. Slight diagonal tilt in the palette to keep the eye moving.
Lighting:       Single warm key from the right, low angle. Strong falloff. The brush tip and the pool of gold are the brightest two points in the frame.
Mood:           The quiet moment before the next brushstroke. Decisions are being made on the palette before they ever reach the canvas.
Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, scholarly fantasy still-life. Chardin meets a contemporary studio painter at 11pm.
Negative:       no faces, no full figure, no AI-cliché glow, no sparkles, no rainbow palette, no plastic or photo-real rendering, no modern brushes with metal ferrules visible at the foreground.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Place immediately before the final CTA section "If you have a character waiting."

Subject:        A single brushed-gold coin, hand-engraved, lying flat on a folded corner of parchment. The coin shows the abstract suggestion of a quill-and-circle motif but is not legible as currency. Beside it sits the corner of a sealed envelope in deep ox-blood wax — no full seal visible, just the edge — implying a quote just signed and ready to send. A trailing curl of beeswax smoke rises from the lower-right edge of the frame.
Typography:     None.
Background:     Parchment-warm. The whole canvas is the texture of aged paper, lit warmly from the upper-left.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 dominant, Gold #D4A24C on the coin, Crimson #B23A2A on the wax corner, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadow under the coin.
Composition:    Strong negative space. Coin and wax corner sit in the lower-left third; upper-right two-thirds is empty warm Parchment. The eye lands on the coin first, then drifts to the wax corner, then out into the space — a "closing chord" feel.
Lighting:       Warm key from upper-left, soft and diffuse. The coin catches a rim of brighter gold along its upper edge. Wax has a single highlight where it caught the light when it was still soft.
Mood:           The decision is made. The math worked. The painting is about to begin.
Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, still-life in the Cartographer's Hand aesthetic.
Negative:       no full coin face with letters or numbers, no modern currency, no skulls, no scrollwork, no full wax seal with sigil, no sparkles, no AI-cliché lighting, no clipart aesthetic.
