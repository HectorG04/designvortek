# Image prompts — Commission Print Delivery

Three image slots, generated against the Cartographer's Hand brand aesthetic. Cover is text-first; inline images are typography-free.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A framed character portrait propped on a small wooden easel beside a candlelit writing desk. The framed piece shows a three-quarter view of a fantasy character (painterly oil-painting style, warm rim light catching the jaw, deep ox-blood collar) sitting under museum glass with a wide off-white mat. A roll of archival glassine paper and a small brass framer's square rest on the desk. A single beeswax candle in a brass holder throws warm light from the right; the brushed gold frame catches one specular highlight along its top edge.
Typography:     The word **PRINT** set in Cinzel 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, in Gold #D4A24C with subtle illuminated-letter rendering, taking ~40% of canvas width, sitting off-center-left, partially occluded by the easel and the painted character peeking from behind the "R".
Kicker line:    "WHAT THE WALL DESERVES" — set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, color Crimson #B23A2A, single short line directly above the bold word.
Background:     Ink-dark candlelit study at night — deep walnut desk surface, the wall behind dropping into near-black shadow with a faint hint of leather-bound book spines just visible in the upper right. Warm parchment-cream light pools around the candle and falls across the framed print.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8, Ink #16110D, Gold #D4A24C, Crimson #B23A2A. Use Indigo #2C3E5C once only, in the deepest background shadow under the desk.
Composition:    Easel and framed print occupy the right two-thirds of the canvas at slight angle, painted character looking off-frame toward the candle. The typography sits in the left third with the painted easel-leg cutting across the descender of the "R". Strong asymmetric balance — eye moves from kicker → bold word → framed character → candle flame.
Lighting:       Single warm candle source from middle-right, ~3000K, throwing strong shadow toward the lower-left corner. Soft rim of gold on the frame's top edge. Background falls into deep ink shadow.
Mood:           Late-night studio handoff — the framing decision has just been made, the painter has been working for hours, the room smells like beeswax and varnish.
Style:          Oil painting with visible brushwork, soft warm edges, medieval-illumination feel, Frazetta + Klimt + Penguin Classics fantasy cover. Slight hand-painted texture on the parchment-cream highlights.
Negative:       no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim lighting, no lens flares, no sparkles, no stock fantasy poses, no gilded scrollwork ornaments, no faux-parchment border, no d20 dice, no modern UI elements.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A close shot of a painted character portrait being lifted carefully from an open archival shipping tube by two hands. The print itself is visible: a fantasy ranger with deep auburn hair, three-quarter view, painted on warm-cream fine-art rag with a deckled edge just catching the light. The hands hold the print at the edges only, the way a framer would. Tissue paper visible inside the tube.
Typography:     None.
Background:     A clean studio worktable with a sheet of acid-free archival board laid flat. Soft natural window light from upper-left.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8, Ink #16110D, Gold #D4A24C with a touch of muted auburn from the portrait subject. Crimson #B23A2A appears only as a small accent on the studio table — a single thread of red ribbon used to seal the tube.
Composition:    Centered vertical — the hands and print fill the upper two-thirds, the open tube and ribbon sit in the lower third. The portrait inside the print is itself a small painting-within-the-painting.
Lighting:       Single soft window source, slightly cool, falling across the surface of the print. The paper texture is just legible in the highlights. Shadow on the right side of the tube goes warm-ink.
Mood:           The moment the client first touches the print — careful, reverent, slightly nervous, the way you hold something you've been waiting six weeks for.
Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, painterly skin tones on the hands, soft edges, atmospheric. The "painting within the painting" is rendered in the same oil-painting style at higher visible-brushwork density to read as a real print, not a screen image.
Negative:       no plastic gloves, no modern packaging, no Amazon boxes, no cellophane, no lens flares, no AI-cliché purple-orange lighting, no glossy magazine sheen, no over-saturated colors.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        A detail crop — the corner of a museum-mat-framed portrait resting on a dark walnut shelf. Visible: the gold rabbet of a slim wooden frame, a wide off-white mat (Crescent Ivory tone), and the deckled edge of the fine-art rag print just kissing the inside of the mat opening. One painted character eye is just visible at the upper edge of the crop — enough to suggest a face without revealing it.
Typography:     None.
Background:     Deep ink-shadow wall behind the shelf. Strong negative space in the lower-right quadrant. A single point of warm light catches the corner of the frame.
Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant, Parchment #F4ECD8 in the mat, Gold #D4A24C on the frame rabbet, a single warm specular highlight at the corner.
Composition:    Strong asymmetry — frame corner in the upper-left, deep shadow filling the lower-right, the painted eye sitting at the focal point along the rule-of-thirds intersection. The composition feels like a closing chord, not a flourish.
Lighting:       Single warm point source from upper-right, ~3000K, throwing one diagonal shadow line across the shelf surface. Most of the frame is in shadow; only the corner reads brightly.
Mood:           After the framer has finished the job, the print is hung, and the painter sees it for the first time on the client's wall. Quiet, final, earned.
Style:          Oil painting, soft warm edges, brushwork visible in the deep shadows. Painterly approach to the mat texture — the cream tone has just enough painted irregularity to read as a real object, not a render.
Negative:       no harsh geometric shadows, no flat color blocks, no AI render sheen, no chrome reflections, no purple-orange lighting, no sparkles, no full character face, no decorative scrollwork on the frame.
