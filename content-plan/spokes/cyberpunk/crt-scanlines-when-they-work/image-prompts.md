# Image prompts — When CRT scanlines work in cyberpunk art

Three prompts for this spoke. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **a single neon-pink (`#FF2D9C`) rim light on the character's profile, breaking the palette intentionally**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A cyberpunk character in three-quarter profile, half-occluded by the bold word. Late twenties, RED-era netrunner aesthetic. Slim build, hooded sweatshirt, a neural cable running from a neck-jack to a deck slung at the hip. The deck itself faces the camera — a small bright rectangular display visible at the lower edge of the frame, showing a faint scanline-and-aberration texture on its tiny screen only. The character's face stays clean painterly oil — no scanlines on the skin. One human eye looking off-frame, the other a Kiroshi-style optic catching warm light.

Typography:     The single word **SCANLINES** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Gold #D4A24C with a subtle Ink #16110D inner shadow. Important: the typography itself has a very faint hint of horizontal scanline structure baked into the letterforms — visible only at full resolution, vanishing at thumbnail — as a visual pun. Word occupies roughly 55% of the canvas width, weighted left-of-centre. The character's profile peeks from behind the right side of the final letter.

Kicker line:    "WHEN THE FILTER EARNS IT" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line.

Background:     Ink-dark — a dim painted interior suggesting a netrunner's hideout. No signage. A faint warm Gold pool of light from off-frame upper-left raking down across the character. The background itself stays clean oil painting; the only scanline texture in the whole image is on the deck's tiny display and faintly in the wordmark.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (faint highlight on the cheekbone), Ink #16110D (background, hood shadow), Gold #D4A24C (typography, warm key, the small deck display reads with a Gold-tinted scanline structure), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text only). Plus the genre-break neon-pink #FF2D9C — used once, as a thin rim along the character's right profile.

Composition:    Asymmetric. Character angled toward the right third, deck visible in the lower-right quadrant. Bold SCANLINES word weighted left, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule under the kicker. The deck's small bright rectangle is a secondary focal point — the eye lands on the wordmark, then the face, then the deck display.

Lighting:       Two-source. Warm Gold key from upper-left raking across the face and hands. Neon-pink rim from upper-right hitting only the silhouette edge. Cool Ink ambient fill everywhere else. The deck display self-illuminates with its own faint cool Gold-cyan glow — tiny, contained, visibly textured.

Mood:           The painting is clean. The screen inside the painting is textured. That's the thesis.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork on character and environment. The scanline texture appears ONLY on the deck's display and very faintly in the wordmark — nowhere else. Frazetta-meets-Syd Mead for the painted base.

Negative:       no whole-image scanline overlay, no full-image chromatic aberration, no VHS dropout effects on the character or environment, no purple-orange dual rim cliché, no holographic billboards, no rain, no katana, no full chrome face, no glowing eyes beyond the single Kiroshi reflex, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no logos, no Cyberpunk 2077 character likeness, no V, no Johnny Silverhand, no synthwave gridlines, no vaporwave aesthetic defaults.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A cyberpunk character framed deliberately as if seen through a security-feed monitor. Mid-thirties, street samurai aesthetic — tactical jacket, chrome forearm, scanning off-frame. Inside the painting itself, a faint suggestion of a monitor frame at the very edges of the image — soft rounded corners, a barely-visible timestamp in the upper-right ("23:47:11"), and a deliberate but subtle scanline structure across the whole image. The conceit: this entire painting IS the screen, by design. Surface treatment is structural, not added.

Background:     A dim painted alley behind the figure, with the suggestion of patched concrete and a single warm Gold light source upper-left. The scanline structure is consistent across foreground and background — confirming the conceit that we're looking at the security feed, not at the character directly.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominating the deep shadows, Gold #D4A24C in the warm key and the faint timestamp, Parchment #F4ECD8 in the cool highlight on the chrome arm, Crimson #B23A2A only in the timestamp text. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as a faint colour cast over the whole image — the security-feed monitor tint. No neon-pink — the genre accent stays on the cover.

Composition:    Center-weighted, slightly low angle as if mounted in a corner. Breathing room around the figure. The timestamp lives in the upper-right corner. The scanline structure is medium-dosed — visible but not dominant. No typography overlay beyond the diegetic timestamp.

Lighting:       Warm Gold key from upper-left, raking across the chrome arm and the jacket shoulder. Cool ambient fill everywhere else. The scanline structure flattens the volumes slightly — which is correct, that's what a real CRT scan does, and the painting earns it because the painting IS the screen.

Mood:           Someone is watching this character through a feed. The character does not know.

Style:          Oil painting with deliberate, integrated CRT scanline texture as part of the conceit. The painting underneath is painterly; the surface treatment is structural. NOT a filter applied at the end — the scanline is the medium.

Negative:       no chromatic aberration on the character (subtle CRT scanline only — no red/cyan fringing), no VHS dropouts, no whole-screen glitching, no neon-everywhere, no holographic anything, no Cyberpunk 2077 wardrobe, no V, no katana, no spread-out weapons display, no second figure visible, no typography beyond the diegetic timestamp, no synthwave gridlines.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a netrunner's small deck display, viewed at close range from above. The deck is a worn matte-black slab; the bright rectangular display fills most of the frame. On the display, a faint scrolling readout — illegible text in a Parchment-tinted Gold against a deep Ink background, with a clean medium-dosed horizontal scanline structure and a barely-visible chromatic-aberration fringe at the brightest edges of the text. The painted hand of the netrunner is just visible at the lower edge, two fingers resting on the deck's bezel — but the hand is rendered in clean painterly oil with NO scanline texture, contrasting with the display.

Background:     Negative space — cool Ink shadow above and around the deck, with a faint warm Gold rim light from off-frame upper-right catching the edge of the deck's casing.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominating, Gold #D4A24C as the scrolling readout text and the warm rim light on the casing, Parchment #F4ECD8 as the brightest specular on the bezel. A faint cool cyan (custom — Indigo #2C3E5C pushed brighter and cooler) used ONLY as the chromatic-aberration fringe at the readout's high-contrast edges. No Crimson. No neon-pink.

Composition:    Deck and display fill the upper two-thirds. Hand and bezel visible in the lower-right. Strong negative space upper-left. The contrast between the textured display and the clean painted hand is the entire point of the image — that's the article's thesis in one square crop.

Lighting:       Soft cool ambient from above, with a warm directional accent on the deck casing edge. The display self-illuminates with its own cool Gold-cyan glow. The hand picks up only the ambient light — clean and unfiltered.

Mood:           Closing chord. The screen is textured because the screen is a screen. The painting around it is paint.

Style:          Oil painting on the casing and the hand, with the display's interior rendered with deliberate CRT scanline + faint chromatic aberration as a contained, diegetic surface treatment. The contrast is the message.

Negative:       no whole-image scanline overlay, no VHS grain on the hand or casing, no glowing veins, no smoke, no purple-orange rim, no typography labels, no second hand visible, no AI-cliché "hero shot" composition, no Cyberpunk 2077 prop design, no synthwave gridlines, no vaporwave palette defaults, no LED-blue everything.
