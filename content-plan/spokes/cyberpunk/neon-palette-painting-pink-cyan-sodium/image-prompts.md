# Image prompts — Neon palette painting: pink, cyan, and sodium yellow

Three prompts for this spoke. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **a single neon-pink (`#FF2D9C`) rim light on the character's profile, breaking the palette intentionally**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A cyberpunk fixer in three-quarter profile, half-occluded by the bold word. Late thirties, sharp-jawed, hair slicked back. A single small chrome implant at the temple — minimal, designer rather than industrial. Tailored mid-weight jacket over a high-collar shirt. Skin tone reads as warm, human, lit by a true Gold key light from camera-left. The neon-pink rim light catches only the right edge of his jaw and the curve of his ear. The pink does not cross onto the front of his face — exactly the rule the article teaches.

Typography:     The single word **NEON** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Gold #D4A24C with subtle Ink #16110D inner shadow. Word occupies roughly 45% of the canvas, weighted left. The fixer's profile peeks from behind the right side of the "N". The neon-pink rim threads behind the typography on the figure side, never touching the letterform itself.

Kicker line:    "PICK TWO OF THREE" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word.

Background:     Ink-dark — a nondescript late-night street wall. Faint suggestion of a sodium-yellow streetlamp glow in the upper-left corner of the frame, off-canvas, providing the warm key motivation. No visible signage, no rain, no holographic billboards. The cyan is implied as ambient cool fill rather than directly shown.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker rule, faint specular on the chrome temple piece), Ink #16110D (background, jacket shadow side), Gold #D4A24C (typography, sodium-yellow key light on the face), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text), plus genre-break neon-pink #FF2D9C — restricted to the silhouette rim on the right side of the figure only.

Composition:    Asymmetric. Fixer's head and shoulders weighted to the right third. Bold NEON word weighted left. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule under the kicker.

Lighting:       Sodium-yellow warm key from upper-left (the unseen streetlamp). Neon-pink rim from upper-right hitting only the silhouette edge. Cool Ink ambient fill across the shadow side. The painting deliberately demonstrates the article's central technique — two neons restrained, no cyan present.

Mood:           He's just stepped out of a meeting that didn't go the way he wanted. Composed. Thinking.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, painterly. The neon-pink rendered as paint not as a digital glow — visible brushstrokes along the edge, slight bleed where the brush dragged.

Negative:       no neon flooding the front of the face, no full-pink wash, no cyan ambient, no holographic billboards, no rain, no chromatic aberration on the cover (despite the topic), no scanlines, no purple-orange dual rim cliché, no V, no Johnny Silverhand, no Cyberpunk 2077 character likeness, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A cyberpunk rockerboy in three-quarter view. Mid-twenties, leather jacket open over a tank top, a chrome plug at the base of the neck just visible, hands resting on a guitar slung across the body. Hair dyed a near-black with one streak of natural rust-red. Standing on a small stage, off-frame audience implied. Skin lit warm, with the neon-pink only on the rim. The article calls this the "club scene exception" — full neon allowed because the brief justifies it. This image shows the controlled version, not the exception.

Background:     Stage backdrop in deep Ink — heavy black with the faintest cyan ambient fill from off-frame stage lighting. A single sodium-warm spotlight catches the rockerboy from upper-left. The neon-pink rim from upper-right reads as a stage light positioned for him alone.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant. Gold #D4A24C as the warm sodium key. Cyan ambient (very desaturated, around #2A4E5A — closer to Indigo #2C3E5C than full electric cyan) used sparingly in the deepest stage shadows. Neon-pink #FF2D9C as the rim only — silhouette edge of the figure, the guitar's headstock, the curve of the cheekbone-but-not-front.

Composition:    Center-weighted. Eye level. The guitar's body fills the lower third. The character's face is upper-centre, lit on the warm-key side, rimmed pink on the cold side. No typography.

Lighting:       Three sources demonstrated in clean discipline. Warm Gold key from camera-left. Neon-pink rim from camera-right (silhouette only). Cool desaturated-cyan ambient fill from low front. The painting is a visual lesson in the article's two-of-three rule.

Mood:           Mid-song, between vocal lines. He is exactly where he is supposed to be.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, painterly. Atmospheric but not foggy. The brushwork on the guitar's lacquer should read as deliberate — wet-on-wet streaks for the highlights.

Negative:       no full-pink wash on the front of the face, no chromatic aberration, no scanlines, no holographic effects, no laser beams, no smoke machine haze that obscures the figure, no audience members visible, no typography, no lens flares, no V, no recognisable 2077 likeness.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a single eye. The iris is a warm human hazel, the white slightly bloodshot. A tiny cyberoptic ring sits at the outer edge of the iris, a thin chrome line catching one specular highlight. Reflected in the wet of the eye is a faint, abstracted suggestion of a neon-pink shape — the article's "single placed accent" rule rendered as the only pink element in the entire close image. The skin around the eye shows visible brushwork, with warm sodium light catching the upper eyelid and cool Ink shadow in the lower lid crease.

Background:     Negative space — Ink-dark falling into a deep warm-grey edge, no other elements.

Palette:        Ink #16110D as the dominant base. Gold #D4A24C as the warm key on the eyelid. Parchment #F4ECD8 as the specular on the chrome cyberoptic ring. Neon-pink #FF2D9C used exactly once, as a tiny reflection in the wet of the eye — the only saturated colour in the frame.

Composition:    Eye centered slightly left of frame, lashes and brow cropped into the composition. The chrome ring catches the brightest highlight. The pink reflection sits as a secondary focal point in the eye's wet — the placed accent the article teaches.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-left. The chrome catches the brightest specular. The pupil reads as deep Ink. The brow shadow falls heavy across the upper third of the frame.

Mood:           Closing chord. Restraint. The smallest possible neon presence that still reads as cyberpunk.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on skin texture, smoother rendering on chrome and the wet of the eye. Quiet.

Negative:       no full-pink wash, no glowing iris, no Hollywood cyberoptic, no laser-blue cybereye glow, no chromatic aberration, no scanlines, no typography, no second eye visible, no AI-cliché "intense gaze" composition.
