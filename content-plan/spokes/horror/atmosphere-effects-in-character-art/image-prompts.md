# Image prompts — Atmosphere effects in character art

Three prompts. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec. Horror genre accent: **the bold word is fraying / partially dissolved at the edges, with one letter trailing a tendril of smoke**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A cleric in dark robes seated in a stone chapel, three-quarter view, head bowed slightly. She holds a brass censer on a thin chain in her right hand. Low fog pools around the bottom of her robes and the chapel floor, thinning at the level of her elbow. Behind her, a single tall arched window throws a pale cool light from the upper-left across her shoulder. A candle on a stone ledge to her right throws warm light onto her hands and the censer's chain. The painting captures the moment before she stands.

Typography:     The single word **COLD** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Ink #16110D with a subtle Crimson #B23A2A inner glow as if printed in old ink that has begun to bleed. The right side of the word is *fraying* — the descender of the D dissolves into a thin tendril of smoke that trails downward and to the right, mixing with the low fog. Word occupies roughly 38% of the canvas width, positioned slightly left-of-centre vertically.

Kicker line:    "FOG, CANDLE, MOTHLIGHT, SMOKE" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark stone chapel interior at the hour before dawn. The arched window behind and to the left of the cleric is the source of the cool mothlight — pale blue-grey, almost colourless, just enough to read as cold. The candle on the stone ledge throws a warm orange-amber pool that catches the censer and the cleric's hands. The far end of the chapel dies into shadow. Stone columns visible only as silhouette.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker line, faint highlight on the censer chain), Ink #16110D (background, robe darks, deep shadow), Gold #D4A24C (candle warmth, censer body, typography fraying edge), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, faint glow within the bleeding ink of the headline). Cool Indigo #2C3E5C used ONCE — as the deep tone in the mothlight wash on the cleric's far shoulder. Avoid green. Avoid neon. Avoid pure black.

Composition:    Asymmetric. Cleric offset to the right of centre, head bowed, eyes downcast. Bold COLD word weighted left of centre, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The smoke tendril from the D is the genre flourish — painted as actual atmosphere mixing into the low fog at the bottom of the canvas, not a digital wisp.

Lighting:       Two-source cross-light. Warm candle key from the lower-right (out of frame or just at the edge of frame). Cool mothlight from the window at the upper-left. The cleric is lit on the warm side of her face by candle, on the cool side by mothlight, with deep shadow falling between the two. The censer catches the brightest highlight in the painting. The fog at the floor catches a faint blue-grey from the mothlight.

Mood:           She has been in the chapel since hours before anyone else. The censer hasn't been swung yet. The candle has burned down to the last inch. The window is showing the first cool grey of an hour that isn't morning yet.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, painterly. Sargent-meets-La Tour. Candle-and-window cross-lighting rendered with classical discipline. The fraying of the typography rendered as actual painted smoke and air, not digital effect.

Negative:       no tentacles, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no purple-orange dual rim light, no lens flares, no sparkles, no glowing eyes, no green glow, no neon, no D&D iconography, no holy-symbol clichés, no obvious horror staging, no wide horror-movie eyes, no stained-glass-cliché lighting, no cathedral-tourism reference photography.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A man seated at a wooden desk at 3am, reading a leather-bound book by candlelight. Three-quarter view from above. He is in his late forties, robe-clad, hair slightly disheveled. One hand rests on the page, the other shields the candle flame in the lower-left of the frame. His face is half-warm, half in shadow. The cleric's crisis-of-faith register — composed but tired.

Background:     A study with a single small leaded window in the upper-right of the frame, throwing a faint cool mothlight onto his left shoulder. The far wall is unreadable shadow. A second book lies closed on the desk, dust visible on the cover. A pewter cup sits in the lower-right, empty.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm Gold #D4A24C across hands, book, and face on the candle side, Parchment #F4ECD8 for the highlights on the page, Crimson #B23A2A as a faint glow in the candle flame and on the page edge. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as a single accent on the mothlit shoulder.

Composition:    Center-weighted. Head and shoulders crop with the book and candle visible in the lower-third focal area. His face sits in the upper-centre, tilted down toward the page. The window glow on his left shoulder is the second-read element — the viewer sees the candle first, the window second.

Lighting:       Two-source cross-light — warm candle from the lower-left, cool mothlight from the upper-right window. Sharp falloff on the warm side. Soft falloff on the cool side. Hands and book are the brightest. Face is a stop darker. Background two stops darker.

Mood:           He has been at this for hours. He was supposed to find an answer in the book and has not. The candle has another twenty minutes in it. He knows.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. La Tour with looser brushwork. Period-grounded realism rather than fantasy stylisation. No typography.

Negative:       no glowing pages, no magical runes, no spell effects, no halos, no fantasy lighting, no purple-orange dual rim, no lens flares, no green glow, no neon, no horror staging, no wide-eyed expression, no stylised hair, no anime stylisation, no typography, no clutter.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a single candle wick, recently extinguished, with one thin tendril of smoke rising from it. The wick is darkened, the wax around it cooling but still slightly glossy. The candle itself is the lower third of the frame. A finger and thumb are just visible at the upper-right edge of frame, having pinched the flame out.

Background:     The negative space around the candle dissolves into warm umber shadow on all sides. A faint cool blue mothlight grazes the top-left of the canvas — a window somewhere out of frame. The smoke tendril rises against the boundary between warm shadow and cool mothlight, catching both temperatures.

Palette:        Ink #16110D in the deep shadow, Gold #D4A24C in the wax and the warm shadow under the candle, Parchment #F4ECD8 as the highlight on the cooling wax, Crimson #B23A2A as a faint residual glow in the wick. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as the mothlight grazing the upper-left corner. The smoke is rendered in warm grey shifting to cool grey as it rises.

Composition:    Candle centred-low, smoke tendril rising vertically through the centre of the upper two-thirds of the canvas. The fingers in the upper-right corner are deliberately partial — only a fragment visible. Strong negative space around the smoke. One element dominates: the tendril of smoke as a final mark on the painting.

Lighting:       The candle is out. The residual heat is visible only in the slight glow at the base of the wick. Warm light comes from somewhere out of frame to the lower-right (an oil lamp, maybe). Cool mothlight comes from the upper-left window. The smoke catches both lights as it rises.

Mood:           Closing chord. The painting in the article is over. The character has put the candle out and is sitting in the new dark. The smoke is the last thing that happened in the room.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, smooth rendering on the wax and wick, soft painterly handling on the smoke. The smoke is the brushwork showpiece — long tapered strokes, opaque at the base, dissolving at the top.

Negative:       no flames, no fire, no glow halos, no horror staging, no typography, no extra candles, no clutter, no Photoshop sharpening, no chromatic aberration, no magical-looking smoke, no swirls, no faces in the smoke, no skull silhouettes, no neon, no green tint.
