# Image prompts — Eldritch horror design

Three prompts. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec. Horror genre accent: **the bold word is fraying / partially dissolved at the edges, with one letter trailing a tendril of smoke**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A woman in late-1920s dress sitting in a leather wingback chair, three-quarter view, half-occluded by the bold word. She is holding a porcelain teacup in her right hand. Her left hand is webbed between thumb and forefinger — barely visible, requires a second look. Her expression is composed, even pleasant. Behind her on the wall, a bookshelf whose perspective lines disagree with the rest of the room by a few degrees, but the disagreement is subtle enough that the eye registers unease before identifying cause.

Typography:     The single word **WRONG** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Ink #16110D with subtle Crimson #B23A2A inner glow as if printed in old ink that has started to bleed into the page. The right edge of the word is *fraying* — the final stroke of the G dissolves into a thin tendril of smoke that trails off-canvas to the upper right. Word occupies roughly 40% of the canvas width, positioned slightly left-of-centre vertically. The woman sits behind and to the right of the word; her teacup is visible in front of the lower curve of the R.

Kicker line:    "A PAINTER'S GUIDE TO COSMIC HORROR" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — a Boston study at evening. Deep umber wood-grain walls in heavy shadow. The bookshelf behind her is the wrong element — the books are correctly painted, the shelves are correctly painted, but the vanishing point of the shelves does not agree with the vanishing point of the rest of the room. The disagreement is small, about three degrees. A single oil lamp on a side table throws warm pool light on her hand and the teacup. The rest of the room dies into shadow.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker accent line, gold-leaf detail on the lamp base), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of figure), Gold #D4A24C (lamp warmth, typography fraying edge), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, faint glow within the bleeding ink of the headline). Avoid green. Avoid neon. Use no Indigo on this cover.

Composition:    Asymmetric. Woman offset to the right third, looking past the typography toward something off-frame upper-left — a thousand-yard composed stare, not horror-movie wide eyes. Bold WRONG word weighted left, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The smoke tendril from the G is the genre flourish — thin, painted as actual atmosphere, not Photoshop wisps.

Lighting:       Single warm key from the unseen lamp at the lower-right, cool Ink ambient fill on the rest of the room. The teacup catches the brightest highlight. Her face is half-lit, half in shadow. The bookshelf is in mid-tone — well enough lit to read its perspective, dim enough to feel like the eye keeps slipping off it.

Mood:           A drawing room at 9pm. She has invited the painter to tea. The painter has noticed three things about the room and is now noticing the fourth. She has not noticed any of them.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, painterly. Sargent-meets-Caravaggio with one subtle architectural error. The fraying of the typography rendered as actual painted smoke, not digital effect.

Negative:       no tentacles, no octopus heads, no green glow, no neon, no glowing eyes, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no Cthulhu silhouette, no eldritch symbols floating in the air, no purple-orange dual rim light, no lens flares, no sparkles, no obvious horror staging, no wide horror-movie eyes, no robe-and-hood cultist stereotypes, no Lovecraft-cliché yellow palette.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A middle-aged fisherman in late-1920s dress, three-quarter view, seated at a small kitchen table. Heavy wool sweater, the cuff of which sits oddly on his wrist — too many folds for the shape inside it, like the cuff is covering a wrist that isn't quite the right shape. He is holding a coffee cup in both hands. The webbing between his thumb and forefinger is visible. His face is calm, lined, weathered by years at sea. His eyes hold a reflection point that's one millimetre higher than physics would suggest.

Background:     A small fisherman's cottage kitchen. A window behind him shows a grey overcast sea. A kerosene lamp on the table throws warm pool light on the cup and his hands. The rest of the room falls into cool shadow. On the table, a half-eaten piece of bread on a plate.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm Gold #D4A24C across hands, cup, and table centre, Parchment #F4ECD8 for the highlights on his face and the sea sky in the window. Crimson #B23A2A used nowhere. No green. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as a single accent in the deep shadow of the room corner.

Composition:    Center-weighted, head-and-shoulders crop. The hands and cup occupy the lower-third focal point. His face sits in the upper-centre. The window is visible to his left, sea visible past him. No typography. The cuff anomaly sits where the eye naturally lands after the cup — the second thing the viewer sees.

Lighting:       Single point source from the kerosene lamp on the table. Sharp falloff. Hands and cup are the brightest elements. Face is a stop darker. Background two stops darker. Classic lantern-light rendering.

Mood:           He has come in from the boat for the last time tonight. He is having coffee before bed. He knows what he is. The painting knows what he is. Nothing in the painting announces it.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Sargent-meets-Wyeth — period-grounded realism rather than horror pastiche.

Negative:       no tentacles visible, no fish features visible, no gill slits, no obvious hybrid features, no green skin, no glowing eyes, no horror staging, no exaggerated expression, no nautical clutter, no scrimshaw, no kraken silhouette in the window, no typography, no lens flares.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a single human eye, painted in extreme close-up. The iris is hazel, brown-flecked, correct in colour. The reflection in the eye is the *load-bearing* element: it shows a room that is not the room the figure is in. The reflection shows a bookshelf, but the bookshelf has one too many shelves than the bookshelf in any of the article's other images. The viewer has to look twice to register the inconsistency. The pupil is slightly dilated for the implied lighting. The eyelashes are painted with the soft brushwork of the rest of the studio's work.

Background:     Negative space — the rest of the face fades into soft shadow on all sides of the eye. The crop is tight enough that only the eye and a fragment of brow and cheekbone are visible.

Palette:        Ink #16110D for the lash line and the deep iris flecks, Parchment #F4ECD8 for the highlights of the eye-white, Gold #D4A24C as the warmest tone in the iris, Crimson #B23A2A as a faint reflected glow from somewhere off-canvas — used to suggest a fire or a lamp the figure is looking at.

Composition:    Eye centred, slight downward tilt of the head implied by the angle of the lashes. The reflection in the iris is the focal point. Strong negative space around the eye. One element dominates: the reflection.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-left, raking across the brow. The eye holds a single specular highlight that's *just* off-axis — placed one millimetre higher than physics would predict for the implied light source. The viewer won't catch this consciously. They will know something is wrong.

Mood:           Closing chord. The character has seen something. The painting shows you only the thing reflected in the eye, and that thing is wrong.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, smooth rendering on the eye itself, visible brushwork on the skin around it. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space.

Negative:       no glowing iris, no extra pupil, no obvious horror staging, no tentacle reflected in the eye, no green tint, no neon, no chromatic aberration, no Photoshop sharpening, no typography, no second eye visible, no AI-cliché "magical eye" composition.
