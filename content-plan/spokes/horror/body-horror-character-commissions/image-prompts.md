# Image prompts — Body horror commissions

Three prompts. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec. Horror genre accent: **the bold word frays at one edge, with a single tendril of smoke trailing off a letter**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A young woman in modern dress — heavy cable-knit sweater pulled to the elbow on one arm, a plain t-shirt visible underneath — seated in a wooden chair, three-quarter view, half-occluded by the bold word. Her left hand rests on her knee, perfectly normal. Her right forearm, exposed, shows the transformation: one small patch of skin on the inside of the wrist is the wrong colour and texture — slightly darker, slightly matte, painted with the dryness of a frog's belly against the lived-in softness of the rest of her arm. The transformation is small, contained, and clearly anatomical rather than gory. Her face is calm, thoughtful, looking past the typography. No blood. No tearing. No drama.

Typography:     The single word **DIAL** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Ink #16110D with subtle Crimson #B23A2A inner bleed as if printed in old ink that has started to spread. The right edge of the L is *fraying* — the bottom stroke dissolves into a thin tendril of smoke that trails off-canvas to the lower right. Word occupies roughly 38% of the canvas width, positioned upper-middle third.

Kicker line:    "WHEN TO PUSH, WHEN TO PULL BACK" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — a modern apartment study at evening. A reading lamp out of frame on the upper-right throws warm pool light on her arm and the chair. Beyond her, the room dies into shadow. A bookshelf is faintly visible in the far background, in mid-tone, suggesting a domestic, lived-in setting rather than a horror set. This is *home*, not a dungeon.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker accent line, the highlights on her sweater), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of figure), Gold #D4A24C (lamp warmth, typography fraying edge), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, faint bleed within the headline ink). The transformation patch uses Indigo #2C3E5C as its dominant tone — a cool wrong colour that doesn't belong in the warm lamp light. Used as the single load-bearing tertiary accent.

Composition:    Asymmetric. Woman offset to the right third. Bold DIAL word weighted left, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The transformation patch on her wrist sits in the negative space between the L's smoke tendril and her body — the eye finds it as the second thing in the image, after the word.

Lighting:       Single warm key from the unseen reading lamp at upper-right, cool Ink ambient fill on the rest of the room. The transformation patch on her wrist catches the warm light like normal skin would *not* — the patch reads cool against warm. The painting works because of the lighting disagreement on that one patch.

Mood:           A Tuesday evening in October. She has been reading. She is now sitting with the painting being made. The transformation has been there for three weeks and she has stopped flinching when she looks at it.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, painterly. Sargent-meets-modern-portrait. The transformation patch rendered with slightly tighter, drier brushwork than the rest of the skin — the painting tells you it is different before the viewer consciously notices the colour.

Negative:       no blood, no gore, no torn skin, no exposed bone, no exposed viscera, no Cronenberg-style ripping, no horror-movie staging, no horror-movie lighting, no purple-orange dual rim light, no green tint, no glowing elements, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no obvious tentacles, no extra eyes visible, no sexualised pose, no pin-up framing, no horror-cliché wide eyes.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A bearded Pathfinder-style alchemist in his workshop, mid-thirties, three-quarter view, seated on a stool at a workbench. Heavy leather apron over period dress, sleeves rolled to the elbow. He is holding a small glass vial in his left hand. His right hand rests on the workbench, palm down. The right hand has six fingers — and the sixth finger is *clearly* growing in, painted slightly thinner than the others, slightly shorter, with the nail not yet fully formed. The skin around the new finger is the same as the rest of the hand. No tearing. No blood. The finger is just there now. His face is tired, resigned, looking at the vial in his other hand.

Background:     A workshop interior at evening. A candle on the workbench throws warm pool light on his hands. Glass vials and small instruments are scattered on the bench in soft focus behind the lit area. The rest of the room dies into shadow. A single window in the far background shows a sliver of night sky.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm Gold #D4A24C across hands, vial, and bench centre, Parchment #F4ECD8 for the highlights on his face and apron. Crimson #B23A2A used in a faint glow inside the vial in his hand — the only saturated colour in the painting. No green. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as a single accent in the night-sky sliver.

Composition:    Center-weighted, slight downward angle as if the painter is standing slightly above the bench. The two hands sit in the lower-third focal pool. The new finger is the visual anchor — placed where the eye naturally lands after the vial. His face is upper-centre, lit but not the focal point.

Lighting:       Single point source from the candle on the workbench. Sharp falloff. The hands and vial are the brightest elements. His face is a stop darker. The background two stops darker. Classic candlelight rendering — the painting respects the physics of a small flame in a large dark room.

Mood:           He has just noticed the sixth finger this morning. He has been alchemy-deep for years and he knows what this means. The painting catches him at the moment of acceptance rather than the moment of horror.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Caravaggio-meets-period-workshop — strong chiaroscuro, deep shadow, single-source warm light. The transformation rendered as subtle and anatomical rather than dramatic.

Negative:       no blood, no torn skin, no obvious horror staging, no glowing potions, no green energy, no neon, no LED-blue, no extra eyes, no tentacles, no symmetric transformation, no Hollywood squint, no typography, no lens flares, no horror-movie wide eyes.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a single hand, palm-down, on a wooden surface. The hand has six fingers. Five are normal, lived-in, with the small nicks and weathering of a working hand. The sixth — the inner one, between the thumb and index — is clearly growing in: shorter than the others by about a third of a length, thinner, the nail bed not yet fully formed, the skin around it is the same as the rest of the hand. There is no tearing, no blood, no joining seam. The finger is simply there. The wrist is visible at the edge of frame, sleeve cuff above it.

Background:     Negative space — the wooden surface fills the lower third, the upper two-thirds fall into dust-warm shadow. The hand is implied to belong to a figure off-frame upper-right; you can just see the lower curve of a leather apron above the cuff.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C as the warm wood tone, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows around the wrist and inside the cuff, Parchment #F4ECD8 as the faintest edge highlight on the fingernails, Crimson #B23A2A nowhere — the image stays calm. No green. No neon. No Indigo.

Composition:    Hand in the lower-centre. Strong negative space upper-left. One element dominates: the new finger, which catches a slightly different highlight than the others — painted with marginally drier brushwork, the way a new fingernail catches light differently than an old one.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-right, raking across the hand. The fingernails of the five existing fingers catch the brightest speculars. The new finger catches its highlight slightly off — a millimetre too high, slightly cooler. The viewer won't catch this consciously. They will know something is wrong.

Mood:           Closing chord. The character is just sitting there. The painting is the moment after he noticed.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on the wood and the cuff, smoother rendering on the fingers themselves. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space.

Negative:       no blood, no tearing, no obvious horror staging, no glow, no green tint, no neon, no chrome, no typography, no second hand visible, no dramatic angle, no AI-cliché "horror hand" composition, no over-rendered veining, no exposed bone.
