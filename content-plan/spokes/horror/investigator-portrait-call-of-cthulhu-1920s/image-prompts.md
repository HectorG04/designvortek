# Image prompts — Investigator portraits, Call of Cthulhu 1920s

Three prompts. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec. Horror genre accent: **the bold word is fraying / partially dissolved at the edges, with one letter trailing a tendril of smoke**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A woman in 1924 dress, mid-twenties to early thirties, seated three-quarter to the viewer in a wingback chair in a quiet study. She wears a dropped-waist day dress in deep ox-blood red over a soft-collared blouse. A cloche hat sits low on her brow. A long ivory cigarette holder rests between two fingers of her right hand, a thin curl of smoke rising. Her left hand rests on a leather-bound book on her lap. Her eyeline is direct to the viewer but her focus sits six inches behind the camera. Composed expression. The painting reads as a photograph from 1924 that has been in a damp box for fifty years.

Typography:     The single word **SEEN** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Ink #16110D with a subtle Crimson #B23A2A inner glow as if printed in old ink that has bled slightly into yellowing paper. The right edge of the word is *fraying* — the final stroke of the N dissolves into a thin tendril of cigarette smoke that trails off-canvas to the upper right, meeting the actual painted smoke from her cigarette holder. Word occupies roughly 40% of the canvas width, sitting slightly above the horizontal centre.

Kicker line:    "A PAINTER'S NOTES ON 1920s INVESTIGATORS" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark study interior. A leaded window behind her left shoulder throws a pale cool light (mothlight) onto her shoulder and the side of her hat. A brass desk lamp out of frame to the lower-right throws a warm pool onto her hands and the book. Far wall is unreadable shadow. A bookshelf is partially visible in the right background, books shown in silhouette. The whole painting reads sepia, mixed not filtered — warm browns, ox-blood, bone-white highlights.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker, faint highlight on the cigarette holder), Ink #16110D (background, deep shadow, hat darks), Gold #D4A24C (lamp warmth on hands and book, typography fraying edge), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, deep ox-blood of the dress, faint glow in the cigarette tip and inside the headline's bleeding ink). Cool Indigo #2C3E5C used ONCE — as the deepest tone in the mothlight wash on her shoulder. Avoid green. Avoid pure black. Avoid clean cool greys.

Composition:    Asymmetric. Woman offset to the right of centre. Bold SEEN word weighted to the left of centre, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The smoke tendril from the N is the genre flourish, painted as a continuation of the actual cigarette smoke — the typography fraying merges with painted atmosphere.

Lighting:       Two-source cross-light. Warm desk-lamp key from the lower-right. Cool mothlight from the window at the upper-left. Her face is lit warm on the right cheek, cool on the left. The book and hands catch the warmest light. The cigarette tip catches a small controlled orange catchlight.

Mood:           She has invited the painter for an afternoon sitting. She has been polite throughout. She has not mentioned what happened in October. The painter has noticed.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, painterly. Sargent meets early Hopper interior. Period-grounded realism with sepia restraint baked into the palette. Edges slightly softer than a contemporary portrait would have. Highlights bloom rather than snap. The fraying of the typography rendered as actual painted smoke, not digital effect.

Negative:       no tentacles, no Cthulhu silhouette, no glowing eyes, no eldritch sigils, no green tint, no neon, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no anachronistic clothing (no modern undercut hair, no plastic accessories, no contemporary makeup, no T-shirt collar), no flapper-with-feather-boa cliché, no 1947 noir lean, no hard cool-blue shadow, no streetlight backlight, no rain on a window, no horror-movie wide eyes, no purple-orange dual rim, no lens flares.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A middle-aged man in 1924 dress, three-quarter view, seated at a small writing desk in a wood-panelled study. He wears a charcoal three-piece suit with a detachable starched white collar and a dark tie. A pocket watch chain runs across the front of his waistcoat. His hands rest flat on a leather-bound manuscript open in front of him. His face is calm and composed. His eyes are looking past the viewer at something in the upper-left of the frame that the viewer cannot see. The eyes are too still. The painting is the antiquarian register from the article.

Background:     A study with a small leaded window in the upper-left, throwing pale mothlight onto his left side. A brass desk lamp out of frame to the lower-right throws warm light onto the manuscript and his hands. Bookshelves visible in the deep background as silhouetted shapes. No props beyond the lamp, watch chain, and manuscript.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the suit and shadow areas, Gold #D4A24C in the warm pool on the manuscript and hands, Parchment #F4ECD8 in the bone-white highlights on the page, Crimson #B23A2A as a faint accent in the warm shadow of the manuscript binding. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C used sparingly in the mothlight on his left shoulder. Skin painted with a slightly higher yellow undertone for the old-photograph read.

Composition:    Center-weighted, head and shoulders with the manuscript visible in the lower-third focal area. His face sits in the upper-centre, eyes looking past the viewer toward upper-left. The watch chain across the waistcoat is the second-read element — the viewer notes the eyes first, the chain second.

Lighting:       Two-source cross-light. Warm desk lamp from the lower-right. Cool mothlight from the upper-left window. Hands and manuscript brightest. Face one stop darker. Background two stops darker. Falloff on the warm side is sharper than the cool side.

Mood:           He has been reading for hours. He has just turned a page. He is no longer fine. The painting does not show why. The eyes do.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, period-grounded realism, atmospheric. Sargent meets Whistler interior. The sepia palette is mixed into the painting from the underpainting. Highlights bloom slightly rather than snap. No typography.

Negative:       no glowing pages, no eldritch sigils, no Mythos creatures visible, no glowing eyes, no extra eyes, no horror staging, no tentacles, no green tint, no neon, no modern fluorescent lighting, no contemporary glasses frames, no modern haircut, no anachronistic props, no typography, no wide horror-movie eyes, no purple-orange dual rim, no lens flares, no chromatic aberration.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a single human eye in extreme close-up, painted in the sepia register of the article. The iris is hazel-brown with subtle ochre flecks. The whites of the eye are slightly bone-toned, not pure white. A small sliver of white is visible above the iris (the "too-wide" cue from the article). The catchlight in the eye sits one millimetre higher than physics would predict for the implied lighting. The eyelashes are painted soft and short. A few faint lines at the outer corner suggest age in the late thirties.

Background:     Negative space — the rest of the face fades into warm umber and ink shadow on all sides of the eye. The crop is tight enough that only the eye and a fragment of brow and cheekbone are visible. A barely-visible warm bone highlight on the cheekbone catches the warm key light from out of frame.

Palette:        Ink #16110D for the lash line and the deep iris flecks, Parchment #F4ECD8 for the bone-toned eye-white highlights, Gold #D4A24C as the warmest tone in the iris, Crimson #B23A2A as a faint reflected glow from the lower-right (suggesting a lamp the figure is sitting near). No cool blues in this crop. Pure sepia register.

Composition:    Eye centred, slight downward tilt of the head implied by the angle of the lashes. The catchlight is the focal point. Strong negative space around the eye. One element dominates: the off-axis catchlight.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from lower-right, raking up across the brow. The eye holds a single specular catchlight that's *just* off-axis from where the implied light source would place it. The viewer won't catch this consciously. They will feel something is wrong.

Mood:           Closing chord. The character has seen something. The painting tells you so by placing the catchlight one millimetre wrong and showing you the sliver of white above the iris. Nothing else is required.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, smooth rendering on the eye, slightly softer brushwork on the skin around it. The sepia register is mixed into the underpainting, not applied as a filter. Highlights bloom rather than snap. Quiet. Trust the negative space.

Negative:       no glowing iris, no extra pupil, no extra eyes visible, no tentacles reflected in the eye, no Mythos creatures, no neon, no green tint, no chromatic aberration, no Photoshop sharpening, no AI-cliché "magical eye" composition, no horror-movie wide eyes, no typography, no second eye visible, no fantasy stylisation.
