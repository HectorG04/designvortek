# Image prompts — Deadlands character art guide

Three prompts for the Deadlands brief spoke. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **a dust-mote light shaft cutting diagonally across the cover, behind the typography**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A Deadlands huckster in three-quarter view, half-occluded by the bold word. Mid-1880s — three-piece wool suit gone to seed, waistcoat unbuttoned at the bottom, shirt collar dark with the work of a long night. Late-thirties, beard trimmed two weeks ago and now growing back. Eyes catching warm light from below — a kerosene lantern set on the same table he's playing at. One hand visible at lower-left, mid-shuffle on a deck of playing cards, one card half-fallen face-up on the table — the Queen of Spades. The hat is a soft-crowned slouch felt, not a Stetson.

Typography:     The single word **HEXEN** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Gold #D4A24C with an Ink #16110D inner shadow as if pressed into the parchment. Word occupies roughly 44% of the canvas width, positioned slightly left-of-centre vertically, weighted toward the upper-middle third. The huckster's face peeks from behind the right side of the "X" and "E" — the typography clearly sits in front of him, with the dust shaft passing behind both.

Kicker line:    "A DEADLANDS BRIEF GUIDE" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — a saloon's back room at the end of a long night. Deep umber wood-grain walls in heavy shadow. A single diagonal dust shaft cuts across the frame from upper-left to lower-right, behind the typography, lighting suspended particles. The shaft is the genre accent — wide, warm amber, with visible motes catching along its edge. The lantern's warm pool is a secondary, smaller light at table level.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker accent line and gold leaf detail only), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of face), Gold #D4A24C (typography, dust-shaft warm core, lantern pool), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, the queen-of-spades' lips, a faint blood-rust glint on a coin at the table's edge).

Composition:    Asymmetric. Huckster's face is offset to the right third, eyes looking past the typography toward something off-frame upper-left. Bold HEXEN word weighted left, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The dust-mote shaft passes diagonally behind everything, anchoring the composition. The fallen Queen of Spades at the lower-left edge is the brightest specific colour in the frame.

Lighting:       Two warm sources — the dust-shaft from upper-left (atmospheric, the painting respects it as ambient evidence of a barn-door opening somewhere off-frame) and the kerosene lantern at lower-mid (the practical key light on his hands and the card). Sharp falloff on both. The right side of his face dies into cool Ink shadow. The hat brim shadow does the work on the upper face.

Mood:           He has just lost a hand he should have won. He is shuffling because he doesn't know what else to do with his hands. The Reckoners are listening. The painter caught him at the worst moment of his evening.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, warm rim light, deep cool shadows. Caravaggio-meets-frontier — strong chiaroscuro, period-grounded realism. Visible canvas tooth. The dust shaft rendered as painted atmosphere, not a Photoshop volumetric.

Negative:       no glowing cards, no green energy, no obvious magical effects, no neon, no LED-blue, no chrome, no Stetson "Cattleman" crease (era-wrong soft hat for an 1880s huckster), no Hollywood squint, no spread-out fan of cards on the table, no second character visible, no purple-orange dual rim light, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no lens flares, no sparkles, no spinning revolver, no smoke.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A Deadlands Harrowed Texas Ranger seated at a writing desk, mid-1880s. Wool sack suit, silver star pinned to the lapel, a long-barrel Colt Single Action Army resting on the desk beside an inkwell. He is mid-thirties, clean-shaven, hair cropped short. The wrongness is subtle — his shadow on the wall behind him falls a few degrees off from where the lantern light predicts, and one iris catches a highlight from a source that isn't visible in the painting. He is writing a report. He has been writing it for hours.

Background:     A boarding-house room at night. A kerosene lantern at upper-left throws warm pool light on the desk, the report, his hands, and the lower half of his face. Beyond him, the room dies into deep cool shadow — only a faint suggestion of wallpaper, the corner of a bedframe, a window with the curtain drawn.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm lantern Gold #D4A24C across hands, desk, and lower face, Crimson #B23A2A on the silver-star's enamel ring and on the wax seal of the closed envelope at the desk's edge. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as the night-air tone in the deepest background — used once, sparingly, as the single tertiary accent.

Composition:    Center-weighted, slight downward angle as if the painter is standing across the desk from him. The report and his writing hand sit in the lower third. His face is in the upper-centre, lit but not the focal point — the hands and the wrong shadow are. Breathing room around the figure. No typography.

Lighting:       Single point source from upper-left (the unseen lantern). Sharp falloff. Hands and report are the brightest element, face is a stop darker, the wrong shadow on the wall is a stop darker again. The painting respects the physics of a small flame in a large dark room except in the one place it doesn't.

Mood:           He has known what he is for six months. He is still writing reports because the job is still the job. Something inside him is reading what he is writing.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Strong chiaroscuro, deep shadow, single-source warm light. Period-grounded realism with one quiet supernatural detail the viewer notices on a second read.

Negative:       no glowing eyes, no obvious magical effects, no neon, no LED-blue, no chrome, no Hollywood squint, no second character visible, no typography, no lens flares, no smoke, no green ghost-rock light, no aggressive supernatural cues.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a hand resting on a worn deck of playing cards on a saloon table. The top card has been turned face-up — the Ace of Spades. The hand is mid-thirties, knuckles scarred from work, a thin scar running across the back of the thumb. A heavy silver ring on the index finger, plain band, no engraving. Beside the deck, a half-empty whiskey glass catching warm lantern light. The cards' edges are soft from years of handling.

Background:     Negative space — saloon-table wood grain in deep warm shadow, only a faint suggestion of the edge of the table beyond the deck. The character is implied off-frame upper-right.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C as the lantern-warmed wood and whiskey tone, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows under the hand and inside the glass's base, Parchment #F4ECD8 only as the faintest edge highlight on the cards' top edges, Crimson #B23A2A on the Ace of Spades' single visible pip — saturated and small.

Composition:    Hand-and-cards in the lower-right third. Strong negative space upper-left. One element dominates: the Ace of Spades, which catches the brightest highlight in the frame and gives the eye somewhere to rest. The silver ring on the index finger catches the second-brightest specular.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-right, raking across the hand and the cards. The ring's silver catches the brightest specular. The whiskey glass's curve catches a second highlight. Shadow falls deep and cool under the hand.

Mood:           Closing chord. The hand has been on this table for hours. The hand will be on this table for hours more. The painting is over.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on the cards and the wood grain, smoother rendering on the silver ring and the whiskey glass. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space.

Negative:       no fingers on the trigger of anything, no spinning cards, no fan, no second hand visible, no glow, no typography, no dramatic angle, no AI-cliché "hero's hand" composition, no smoke, no green light.
