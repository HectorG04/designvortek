# Image prompts — Cowboy fashion across the eras

Three prompts for the western fashion spoke. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **a dust-mote light shaft cutting diagonally across the cover, behind the typography**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A before-after split composition. On the left half, a 1845 mountain man in three-quarter view — long fringed buckskin coat, a soft beaver-felt slouch hat low over the eyes, a powder horn slung on a wide leather strap, full unkempt beard, Hawken rifle leaned against a tree trunk just off-frame. On the right half, an 1885 railroad-town gambler in a black three-piece wool suit, white detachable collar with a thin string tie, bowler hat tilted slightly back, watch chain visible across the waistcoat, clean-shaven with a trimmed moustache. The two figures stand back-to-back, each occluded slightly by the bold word in the centre.

Typography:     The single word **DECADES** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Gold #D4A24C with an Ink #16110D inner shadow as if pressed into the parchment. Word occupies roughly 46% of the canvas width, positioned centred horizontally, weighted toward the upper-middle third. Both painted figures peek from behind the letters — the mountain man's shoulder and hat behind "DEC", the gambler's profile behind "ADES".

Kicker line:    "1840s TO 1900s, WHAT CHANGES" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — an undefined deep umber space that reads as period photographer's studio backdrop. A single diagonal dust shaft cuts across the frame from upper-left to lower-right, behind the typography, lighting suspended particles. The shaft is the genre accent — wide, warm amber, with visible motes catching along its edge. The two figures are lit by warm side light as if standing in front of a tall studio window.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker accent line and gold leaf detail only), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of both figures), Gold #D4A24C (typography, dust-shaft warm core, the gambler's watch chain, the mountain man's brass buckle), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, a single red trade-cloth sash on the mountain man).

Composition:    Symmetric mountain-man / gambler split, with the bold DECADES word as the dividing column. The kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The dust-mote shaft passes diagonally behind everything, anchoring the composition. The mountain man's red sash and the gambler's brass watch chain are the brightest specific colours in the frame.

Lighting:       Warm side light from upper-left (the dust shaft's implied source — a tall window off-frame). Sharp falloff. Both figures' off-side faces die into cool Ink shadow. The hat brim shadows do the work on the upper faces — wide soft slouch shadow on the mountain man, sharp bowler-brim line on the gambler.

Mood:           Forty years between them. Same continent, different country. The painting is asking the viewer to notice the difference.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, warm rim light, deep cool shadows. Period-grounded realism. Strong chiaroscuro. Visible canvas tooth. The dust shaft rendered as painted atmosphere, not a Photoshop volumetric.

Negative:       no Stetson on the mountain man, no buckskin on the gambler, no Hollywood low-slung holsters, no Cattleman-crease Stetson on either figure (era-wrong for both), no Hollywood squint, no spread-out fans of cards, no purple-orange dual rim light, no skulls, no wax seals, no blackletter, no lens flares, no sparkles, no spinning revolvers, no smoke, no neon, no chrome.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A 1878 trail-boss portrait — three-quarter view, mid-fifties, weathered face, full salt-and-pepper beard. He wears a heavy waxed-canvas duster the colour of trail dust, a faded blue cotton bandanna knotted at the throat, a wool flannel shirt visible at the collar, and a tall round-crowned Stetson "Boss of the Plains" with no formal crease — just years of weather shaping it. A Colt Single Action Army with a 7.5" cavalry-length barrel rides high on his hip in a Slim Jim holster. He stands beside a wooden corral fence, late afternoon, with the implied dust of a recent cattle drive settling around his boots.

Background:     Open prairie behind him, deep umber-and-gold under late-afternoon sun. The corral fence cuts across the lower-right. A faint suggestion of livestock in the deep distance, blurred and unimportant. The sky is warm cream-and-gold, no clouds.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C dominant in the warm afternoon light across his duster and the prairie behind him, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows under the hat brim and on the shadow side of the duster, Crimson #B23A2A as a single saturated note — a thin red trim on the bandanna's edge or a small rust mark on the revolver's frame. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C used sparingly as the deepest sky tone above the horizon — the single tertiary accent.

Composition:    Center-weighted, slight upward angle as if the painter is standing slightly below and to the left. The figure fills the upper two-thirds. The corral fence and a glimpse of the ground are in the lower third. Breathing room around the figure. No typography.

Lighting:       Late afternoon golden-hour, warm and directional from the upper-left, raking across his duster and the right side of his face. The hat brim casts a deep shadow over his upper face. The eyes catch a small specular highlight from the warm light. Sharp falloff into deep cool shadow on the right side of his body.

Mood:           He has just brought the herd in. Five weeks on the trail. He is tired in the way a man is tired after work that he chose. The painting catches him for a moment before he goes inside.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Strong warm/cool contrast. Period-grounded realism. Deep Frazetta-warm tones, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light.

Negative:       no glowing anything, no obvious supernatural cues, no neon, no LED-blue, no chrome, no Hollywood squint, no second character visible, no typography, no lens flares, no smoke, no Cattleman crease on the Stetson (era-correct uncreased shape), no low-slung holster, no spinning revolver, no modern denim, no semi-automatic anything.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a 1885 bowler hat resting on a wooden table beside a folded grey wool sack coat, a white detachable shirt collar, and a thin string tie coiled into a loose loop. The bowler is plain black felt with a narrow grosgrain band. The collar shows a small wear-line where it has been worn many times. Beside the hat, a Smith & Wesson Schofield revolver lies on its side, barrel pointing away from the viewer, the top-break latch catching the light. Late evening — the implied owner has just come in from a long day and laid the day's clothes down before sitting somewhere off-frame.

Background:     Negative space — deep warm wood-grain table surface in soft shadow, only a faint suggestion of the table's far edge fading into Ink-dark. The character is implied off-frame upper-right.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C as the lantern-warmed wood and the brass of the Schofield's frame, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows under the hat and inside the folded coat's interior, Parchment #F4ECD8 as the white detachable collar — the brightest element in the frame, Crimson #B23A2A on a single small detail — a faded red maker's mark stamped inside the hat's leather sweatband, just visible at the right angle.

Composition:    Objects clustered in the lower-right two-thirds. Strong negative space upper-left. The white detachable collar catches the brightest highlight and gives the eye somewhere to rest. The Schofield's top-break latch catches the second-brightest specular. The bowler's curve anchors the composition's lower edge.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-right, raking across the table and the laid-out objects. The white collar is the brightest, the brass on the Schofield second, the felt of the bowler is matte and reads soft. Shadow falls deep and cool under the coat's folds.

Mood:           Closing chord. The day is done. The clothes are off. The painting is over.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on the wool sack coat and the wood grain, smoother rendering on the brass and the white collar. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space.

Negative:       no fingers visible, no second figure, no spinning revolver, no glow, no typography, no dramatic angle, no AI-cliché "hero's gear" composition, no smoke, no green light, no neon, no chrome, no LED, no modern five-pocket jeans visible.
