# Image prompts — Weird west: blending horror and frontier

Three prompts for the weird-west craft spoke. Cover follows the Cartographer's Hand spec from `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Genre accent: **a dust-mote light shaft cutting diagonally across the cover, behind the typography**.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        A weird-west sheriff in three-quarter view, half-occluded by the bold word. Mid-1880s — wool sack suit, silver star pinned to the lapel, a string tie at the throat, a wide-brimmed Stetson with a formalised cattleman crease low over the eyes. Late-forties, weathered face, salt-and-pepper moustache, eyes catching warm light from below. He stands in front of a wall-mounted gilt-framed oval mirror that should reflect the back of his head — but doesn't. The mirror shows only the room behind him. The wrongness is small. The viewer registers it on second look.

Typography:     The single word **WRONG** in Cinzel weight 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, set in Gold #D4A24C with an Ink #16110D inner shadow as if pressed into the parchment. Word occupies roughly 42% of the canvas width, positioned slightly right-of-centre, weighted toward the upper-middle third. The sheriff's face peeks from behind the left side of the "W" and "R" — the typography clearly sits in front of him, with the dust shaft passing behind both.

Kicker line:    "WEIRD WEST WITHOUT THE CAMP" set in Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, colour Crimson #B23A2A, sitting directly above the bold word. One short line, no wrap.

Background:     Ink-dark interior — a sheriff's office at the end of a long evening. Deep umber wood-grain walls in heavy shadow. The gilt-framed oval mirror behind him is the painting's quiet horror — visible to one side of his head, clearly empty of his reflection. A single diagonal dust shaft cuts across the frame from upper-left to lower-right, behind the typography, lighting suspended particles. The shaft is the genre accent — wide, warm amber, with visible motes catching along its edge. A kerosene lantern on the desk in front of him is the practical key light.

Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8 (kicker accent line and the cream wall behind the mirror), Ink #16110D (background and shadow side of face), Gold #D4A24C (typography, dust-shaft warm core, lantern pool, the mirror's gilt frame), Crimson #B23A2A (kicker text, the silver star's enamel ring, a single faint blood-rust mark on the desk's surface).

Composition:    Asymmetric. Sheriff's face is offset to the left third, eyes looking past the typography toward something off-frame upper-right. Bold WRONG word weighted right, kicker centred above. A 60×3px Gold #D4A24C accent rule sits just under the kicker. The dust-mote shaft passes diagonally behind everything, anchoring the composition. The empty mirror sits behind his shoulder — the brightest cool note in the frame, almost glowing with its own absence.

Lighting:       Two warm sources — the dust shaft from upper-left (atmospheric, the painting respects it as ambient evidence of a high window opening somewhere off-frame) and the kerosene lantern at lower-mid (the practical key light on his hands, the desk, and the lower half of his face). Sharp falloff on both. The right side of his face dies into cool Ink shadow. The hat brim shadow does the work on the upper face. The mirror catches a faint reflection of the room — but no figure.

Mood:           He has known for six months. The job is still the job. He doesn't look at the mirror anymore. The painter caught him at the moment a viewer would notice.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, warm rim light, deep cool shadows. Caravaggio-meets-frontier — strong chiaroscuro, period-grounded realism. Visible canvas tooth. The dust shaft rendered as painted atmosphere, not a Photoshop volumetric. The mirror's missing reflection rendered with absolute confidence — no painter's trick, just the reflection isn't there.

Negative:       no glowing eyes, no green flames, no lightning, no obvious supernatural effects, no translucent ghost overlays, no skeleton or skull faces, no neon, no LED-blue, no chrome, no Hollywood squint, no spinning revolver, no second character visible, no purple-orange dual rim light, no skulls in the decor, no wax seals, no blackletter, no lens flares, no sparkles, no smoke.

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A weird-west bounty hunter seated at a saloon table, three-quarter view, mid-1880s. Long waxed-canvas duster the colour of trail dust, a heavy wool flannel shirt visible at the collar, a faded blue bandanna at the throat, a tall round-crowned Stetson with a soft individual crease. Mid-forties, full beard, eyes catching warm lantern light. A Colt Single Action Army with a 7.5" barrel rests on the table in front of him, hand laid loosely across the grip. Beside the revolver, a single playing card lies face-up — but the suit is wrong. Not hearts, spades, diamonds, or clubs. A fifth suit, painted with absolute confidence but unfamiliar to the viewer. The character isn't looking at the card. He has known about it for years.

Background:     A saloon at the end of the night, mostly empty. A kerosene lantern hangs above the table, throwing warm pool light on the table surface, the revolver, the card, and the bounty hunter's hands and lower face. Beyond, the room dies into deep cool shadow — only a faint suggestion of a bar's edge, a row of bottles, the corner of a window with the curtain drawn.

Palette:        Ink #16110D dominant in the shadow areas, warm lantern Gold #D4A24C across hands, table, revolver, and lower face, Crimson #B23A2A on the card's wrong fifth-suit symbol — saturated and small, the painting's single most specific colour note. Cool Indigo #2C3E5C as the night-air tone in the deepest background, used once.

Composition:    Center-weighted, slight downward angle as if the painter is standing across the table from him. The revolver and the wrong card sit in the lower third. His face is in the upper-centre, lit but not the focal point — the hand, the gun, and the card are. Breathing room around the figure. No typography.

Lighting:       Single point source from upper-left (the unseen lantern). Sharp falloff. Hands and table are the brightest element, face is a stop darker, the deepest background is a stop darker again. The wrong card catches the lantern light correctly — it isn't supernatural in its lighting, only in its symbol.

Mood:           He has been carrying the card for fifteen years. He has never told anyone. The painting catches him a moment after he laid it on the table for the first time in five years.

Style:          Oil painting, visible brushwork, atmospheric, painterly. Same studio hand as the cover. Strong chiaroscuro, deep shadow, single-source warm light. Period-grounded realism with one quiet supernatural detail the viewer notices on a second read.

Negative:       no glowing card, no obvious magic effects, no green light, no neon, no LED-blue, no chrome, no Hollywood squint, no second character visible, no typography, no lens flares, no smoke, no aggressive supernatural cues, no translucent ghost overlays, no skull imagery on the card itself.

### Inline close (1:1 square, 1080×1080)

Subject:        Detail crop — a hand-tooled leather holster on a polished wooden bar top, a Colt Single Action Army half-drawn from it. Late evening. The hand that placed the gun there is implied off-frame upper-right. The holster's leather catches warm light. The revolver's barrel and frame catch warm light. The character's shadow, where it should fall on the bar top from a high-mounted lantern off-frame, falls in roughly the right direction — but the gun itself casts no shadow at all. The leather casts a shadow. The bar's wood grain casts shadows under its small imperfections. The Colt does not.

Background:     Negative space — saloon bar's wood grain in deep warm shadow, only a faint suggestion of a brass rail along the bar's far edge. The character is implied off-frame upper-right.

Palette:        Gold #D4A24C as the lantern-warmed wood and the brass on the bar's far edge, Ink #16110D in the deepest shadows under the holster and along the gun's underside (where its shadow should be, but isn't), Parchment #F4ECD8 only as the faintest edge highlight on the gun's frame, Crimson #B23A2A on a small detail — a single faded red maker's stamp on the holster's leather, visible at the right angle.

Composition:    Holster and gun in the lower-right two-thirds. Strong negative space upper-left. One element dominates: the place where the gun's shadow should be — a patch of unbroken lantern light on the bar top, slightly too bright, slightly too clean. The eye reads the absence on the second look. The brass on the bar's far edge catches the brightest specular.

Lighting:       Soft warm directional from upper-right, raking across the gun, the leather, and the bar. The leather casts a long soft shadow toward the lower-left. The gun does not. The painting renders this with the same brushwork commitment as everything else in the frame.

Mood:           Closing chord. The gun is on the bar. The shadow is missing. The painting is over.

Style:          Oil painting, detail crop, visible brushwork on the leather and the wood grain, smoother rendering on the gun's blued steel. Quiet. Painterly. Trust the negative space. Trust the absence.

Negative:       no fingers visible, no glowing gun, no translucent effects, no green light, no typography, no dramatic angle, no AI-cliché "hero's gun" composition, no smoke, no neon, no chrome, no LED, no spinning revolver, no second figure.
