# Image prompts — The Outlaw Portrait: building a believable wanted-poster face

Genre accent: **Western** — a dust-mote light shaft cuts diagonally across the cover, behind the typography.

---

### Cover image (16:9, 1920×1080)

Subject:        An outlaw in head-and-shoulders, dead-centre composition mimicking a period wanted-poster photograph — slight three-quarter view, neutral expression, eyes on the viewer with a flat resignation. Late thirties, weathered skin, a single scar across the brow ridge above the right eye. Three-day stubble. A worn coat collar, faintly grimed shirt underneath. The figure is half-occluded by the typography — the bold word sits across the lower third of the painting like a wanted-poster headline, with the figure's chest and lower coat partially behind the letters.
Typography:     The bold word "WANTED" set in Cinzel 700, letter-spacing -0.015em, in Ink #16110D with a faint distress pattern as if printed on cheap 1880s paper. Word takes ~48% of canvas width, sits across the lower third. A faint reward sum "$2,500" set in smaller Inter uppercase 600 below WANTED in Crimson #B23A2A.
Kicker line:    "AN OUTLAW PORTRAIT" — Inter uppercase 600, 0.15em letter-spacing, in Crimson #B23A2A, sitting above the painted figure at the very top of the cover. 60×3px gold accent line in #D4A24C beneath the kicker.
Background:     Parchment-warm — a desaturated bone-and-cream textured backdrop suggesting cheap period printer paper, with very faint foxing at the corners. A diagonal dust-mote shaft cuts from upper-right behind the figure.
Palette:        Parchment #F4ECD8, Ink #16110D, Gold #D4A24C, Crimson #B23A2A, with cool greys and warm umbers in the figure.
Composition:    Symmetric centred figure (this is the wanted-poster tradition), with the bold word breaking the symmetry by sitting slightly right-of-centre in the lower third. The dust-mote shaft cuts diagonally behind both figure and typography.
Lighting:       Flat frontal cool source, mimicking the harsh undirected light of a period studio photograph. Minimal shadow on the face except a faint cool fall-off under the jaw. The painted version warms the source slightly toward Gold #D4A24C to keep the brand palette legible.
Mood:           A face that has been arrested before and will be again. Tired, alert, slightly amused by the inconvenience, not afraid.
Style:          oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, Mathew Brady tintype + Penguin Classics cover, period-photograph weight
Negative:       no blackletter, no wax seals, no skulls, no AI-cliché purple-orange dual rim light, no glowing eyes, no smirk, no Hollywood saloon glamour, no full-face hat shadow obscuring the face (the face must be the painting), no two-revolver hero pose, no horse, no spinning typography effects, no actual recognisable historical person (this is an invented outlaw)

### Inline mid-article (4:5 portrait, 1080×1350)

Subject:        A woman outlaw shoulders-up in three-quarter view, late thirties, wearing a sturdy cotton shirt with sleeves rolled to the elbow under an open vest, hair tied back. Hat in hand at the lower edge of the frame. A small scar at the jaw on the side facing the camera. Sun-weathered skin, tired eyes with a small alert quality, mouth in a neutral expression with the faintest downturn at one corner. She looks past the viewer slightly, not at them. The styling is explicitly Pearl Hart-coded — practical, road-worn, the opposite of the Hollywood saloon-girl trope.
Typography:     None.
Background:     Warm dusty mid-distance, no specific landscape, an out-of-focus horizon line at the lower third. A single thin band of warm late-afternoon light across the upper background.
Palette:        Warm ochre and burnt umber background, Ink #16110D in shadow areas, warm cream cotton shirt with mild brown stains at the collar, dark vest, a single thread of Crimson #B23A2A in a hair ribbon or shirt button.
Composition:    Centred subject, slight asymmetry from the off-camera gaze. Generous breathing room above the head. The hat at the lower frame edge anchors the composition.
Lighting:       Single warm late-afternoon source from frame-right. Subtle rim light on the cheekbone and the curve of the shoulder. Deep cool shadow on the opposite side of the face.
Mood:           Eighteen months on the road. The character has just stopped, taken her hat off, and is looking at something past the viewer she has no intention of explaining.
Style:          oil painting, visible brushwork, soft edges, scholarly portrait register, the opposite of Hollywood saloon-girl glamour
Negative:       no typography, no corset, no garter holster, no low-cut top, no loose tousled hair, no sneer, no Hollywood saloon glamour, no decorative gunbelt, no AI-cliché beauty-filtered skin, no purple-orange rim light, no exaggerated bust, no come-hither expression — this is a working outlaw portrait

A small piece of a pocket watch chain catches the light at the upper edge of the frame, suggesting the watch is tucked into the vest pocket but we cannot see it.
Typography:     None.
Background:     Negative space falling into a warm worn coat fabric texture, slightly darker than the hand. Out of focus.
Palette:        Warm flesh tones with mild road-tan colouring, Ink #16110D in deep shadow, a single Gold #D4A24C catchlight on the watch chain.
Composition:    The hand sits on a rule-of-thirds intersection in the upper third; the watch chain runs diagonally across the upper-right corner. Strong negative space along the lower half.
Lighting:       A single warm low source from frame-left, very soft. The watch chain is the brightest small detail; the hand carries the storytelling weight.
Mood:           A closing chord. The viewer notices the watch chain on second look and starts wondering where the watch came from. The painting refuses to answer.
Style:          oil painting, visible brushwork, tight detail study, the kind of crop that would sit on a gallery card next to the full portrait
Negative:       no typography, no full figure, no glowing magical particles, no dramatic lens flare, no AI-cliché skin smoothing, no exaggerated jewelry, no rings or decoration that breaks the working-outlaw register
